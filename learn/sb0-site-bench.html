Site Bench

Size a home’s or a wind farm’s equipment, run every hour of 2025, and see the cash flow

The brief

The site
A Prairie Town home or the ridge wind farm, behind one meter, through every hour of 2025.
Its hours
The hourly patterns of the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) grid in 2025, reshaped to EIA's monthly capacity factors: modelled sites, not measured ones.
You decide
Home: solar (kW), a battery (kWh), a heat pump, the tariff. Farm: wind, a battery, an electrolyzer, a hydrogen tank.
The engine works out
The cheapest way to run all 8,760 hours, a linear program; then the bills, the CO₂ and the NPV.
It leaves out
Outages, taxes and incentives, storage from one week to the next, and costs that change over time.

Open-ended: start from a preset, change one thing at a time, and copy the plan link to keep a plan.

How the engine works, as diagrams

Decisions
Every hour: how much to buy and send back (home) or sell and spill (farm), what goes into and out of the battery, and what the electrolyzer and tank do.
Goal
The lowest yearly cost for the home, or the highest yearly revenue for the farm, with the sizes you set.
Rules
Each hour balances; nothing runs above its size; the battery loses 15% of what it stores; the line and the buyer's take are limits.
Time
All 8,760 hours of 2025, as 52 weeks and 31 December, one small linear program each.
Tie rule
A tiny wear charge on battery use and, on the farm, a hair's preference for selling earlier in the week. Both are left out of every number shown.
Ties
Every total shown is the same on every least-cost schedule. With storage, the hour-by-hour schedule, and the hour counts read from it, can differ.
It holds when
  • the weather and prices of 2025 are a fair guide to the next 25 or 30 years;
  • the sourced costs, converted to 2025 dollars, hold for the whole horizon;
  • the grid is always there to buy from (home) or to sell to, up to the line (farm).
It leaves out
  • storage carried from one week to the next (the check below measures what that changes);
  • outages, taxes, tax credits, incentives and financing;
  • how other plants respond to the site: the CO₂ uses one average factor (CF6 explains the difference).

Go deeper · ModelerThe linear program behind the bench
  • Each hour adds a balance row. For the home: bought − sent back + battery out − battery in = the home’s use − solar.
  • For the farm: sold + spilled + into the battery + into the electrolyzer − battery out = wind.
  • The battery’s state of charge is one more variable per hour: this hour’s level = last hour’s + 0.85 × charge − discharge.
  • “Size it for me” makes the sizes decisions too, each charged its yearly cost through the capital recovery factor. It runs on a few representative weeks, as lesson 13 and M3 do.
  • Storage and coupled carriers, modelling power, hydrogen and heat together (M4, in the Modeler tier), writes this model in Python.
