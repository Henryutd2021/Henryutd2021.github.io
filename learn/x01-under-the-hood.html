Eight components, the discipline behind each, and what system models borrow

Inverter · Power electronics
Asks
  • How does it turn the direct current of solar panels and batteries into alternating current?
  • How fast can it respond, and does it hold on through a voltage dip?
Models borrow
Its AC rating, conversion losses, ramp speed and, in A17, whether it forms the grid or follows it.
In this course
  • DC to AC (what it does): Solar cells make direct current; an inverter turns it into the grid's alternating current.
Read next
Erickson and Maksimović, Fundamentals of Power Electronics (Springer)
Protective relay · Power system protection
Asks
  • Which breaker should open, and how fast, when a line has a fault?
  • How are its settings chosen so that healthy lines stay in service?
Models borrow
Not the relay settings: equipment ratings, such as a line's thermal rating, and how often equipment is forced out of service.
In this course
  • Thermal rating (a line's limit): The most current a line or transformer can carry before it overheats; models keep flows under it. A relay is different: it detects a fault and tells a breaker to open.
Read next
Horowitz and Phadke, Power System Relaying (Wiley)
Turbine blade · Aerodynamics and structural engineering
Asks
  • How much of the wind's power can the rotor catch?
  • Will the blade survive repeated gusts over its intended life?
Models borrow
A power curve (output at each wind speed) with its rated power, and how often the turbines are available.
In this course
  • 59.3% (the Betz limit): The most of the wind's power any rotor can take, the Betz limit (physics).
  • 0.52 (best measured power coefficient): The best measured power coefficient of DOE's 1.5 MW test turbine: the share of the wind's power it turned into electricity.
Read next
Burton, Jenkins, Sharpe and Bossanyi, Wind Energy Handbook (Wiley)
Gas-turbine combustor · Combustion and thermodynamics
Asks
  • How hot can the flame burn before metal or emission limits are reached?
  • How low can the turbine run and still burn steadily?
Models borrow
Whole-plant numbers it helps set: a heat rate, a ramp limit, a minimum load and a start time.
In this course
  • 7,535 Btu/kWh (whole-plant heat rate, 2025 full-load test): EIA's 2025 average full-load tested heat rate for U.S. natural-gas combined-cycle plants, a whole-plant value the turbines and cooling shape too: about 45.3% of the fuel's energy becomes electricity at full load.
  • 40 MW per hour (whole-plant ramp limit, Prairie Town): The ramp limit of Prairie Town's whole combined-cycle plant (illustrative).
Read next
Turns, An Introduction to Combustion (McGraw-Hill)
Reactor core · Reactor physics, thermal hydraulics and nuclear materials
Asks
  • How does the core hold its chain reaction steady?
  • How is heat carried away so that the fuel and the metal tubes that hold it stay within their limits?
Models borrow
Whole-plant numbers it helps set: a thermal and an electric rating, an outlet temperature and a heat rate.
In this course
  • about 300 °C (outlet temperature): The outlet temperature of large water-cooled reactors.
  • 10,443 Btu/kWh (whole-plant heat rate, 2025): The U.S. nuclear fleet's 2025 heat rate, a whole-plant value the turbine and cooling shape too: about 32.7% of the core's heat becomes electricity.
Read next
Lamarsh and Baratta, Introduction to Nuclear Engineering (Pearson)
Electrolyzer stack · Electrochemistry, catalysis and materials
Asks
  • How much electricity does a kilogram of hydrogen take?
  • How fast do the cells wear, and how low can the stack run?
Models borrow
Electricity per kilogram of hydrogen for the whole system (stack, power supply, pumps and dryers), a minimum load and a stack life.
In this course
  • 57 kWh/kg (whole-system electricity use): A16 assumes about 57 kWh per kilogram of hydrogen for the complete PEM electrolyzer system; stack design helps set that system value.
  • PEM 5%, alkaline 15% (minimum loads): A16 assumes these lowest steady loads for PEM and alkaline electrolyzers, as a share of their size, from IRENA's 2020 overview; real equipment varies.
Read next
U.S. Department of Energy, Hydrogen and Fuel Cell Technologies Office, Hydrogen Production: Electrolysis (energy.gov)
Battery cell · Electrochemistry and materials science
Asks
  • How much energy does a cell hold, and how fast can it charge?
  • How do heat and cycling age it?
Models borrow
A round-trip efficiency for the whole battery system, a duration (energy over power) and a life.
In this course
  • 85% (round trip of the battery system): The course assumes 85% of the electricity sent into its utility-scale battery system comes back out: charging and discharging losses together, for the whole system, not one cell.
  • 15 years (life): The life the course gives that battery system.
Read next
Linden's Handbook of Batteries (McGraw-Hill)
Control room · Control engineering, operations and cybersecurity
Asks
  • How do operators see the grid every few seconds and send each plant its target output?
  • How are those systems kept safe from cyberattack?
Models borrow
Operating rules: how often dispatch runs, how much reserve is held and which limits are watched.
In this course
  • CIP (cybersecurity standards): The mandatory Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) standards of the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC) cover the bulk power system's cyber systems, in general not those on the distribution grid.
Read next
Wood, Wollenberg and Sheblé, Power Generation, Operation, and Control (Wiley)

The kinds of number a model borrows

  • Rating: How much it can make, carry or store: MW, MWh, a line's limit.
  • Efficiency: Output per input, like a round trip; or input per output, like a heat rate or kWh per kilogram (lower is better).
  • Ramp limit: How fast its output can change, in MW per hour.
  • Minimum load: The lowest output it can hold steadily, as a share of its size; below that it must stop.
  • Outlet temperature: How hot the heat it gives is, which sets the uses that heat can serve.
  • Reliability and life: Three separate numbers: how often it fails, how long each outage lasts, and how many years it serves before replacement.
  • Operating rule: How the people and software running the grid decide: time steps, reserves, limits.

What you just did, as diagrams

Disciplines
Power electronics, protection, aerodynamics, combustion, reactor physics, electrochemistry and control each design a part of the system.
What crosses over
A system model borrows ratings, efficiencies, ramp limits, minimum loads, outlet temperatures, and reliability and life, plus the control room's operating rules.
What stays
Designs, materials and calculations stay with each discipline; the model sees them only through the numbers they lead to.
Cybersecurity
NERC's mandatory standards protect the bulk power system's cyber systems; distribution-grid devices are in general outside them.

Check yourself

Two quick checks.

Myth or fact? “To plan a grid, a modeler needs the full design of every turbine and inverter.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. A system model borrows a few numbers from each design (ratings, efficiencies, ramp limits, minimum loads, outlet temperatures, and reliability and life); the design itself stays with the engineers.

An electrolyzer engineer reports the lowest output the stack can hold steadily, as a share of its size. Which kind of number does a system model borrow?

  • Rating
  • Minimum load
  • Ramp limit
Answer

Minimum load: below it the stack must stop. A rating is the most it can take; a ramp limit is how fast its output can change.
