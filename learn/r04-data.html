Log every cleaning choice and show what it changes

Research studio R4 of Energy Systems, Step by Step: clean a real year of hourly grid data (SPP 2025, EIA-930), log what each cleaning choice changes, check data licences and write a data note.

The task

  1. Produce: a cleaning log (rule, count, reason and effect for each step), a licence check of every source, and a data note someone else can rerun from.
  2. From: SPP's hourly file for 2025 (demand, wind, solar; EIA-930, public domain), the starter notebook and, in practice, one spatial layer.
  3. Done when: the rubric's bar for R4's part: a complete audit trail, with licences checked and credit stated. The verification log and a validation come in R5.

Licences: what each source asks of you

  • EIA-930 hourly grid data (this studio's file): Public domain. A U.S. government work; EIA asks for an acknowledgment with the date.
  • U.S. Wind Turbine Database (turbine locations): Public domain. Public domain; the USGS asks for an acknowledgment line.
  • Ember monthly electricity data: Attribution (CC BY). CC BY 4.0: credit Ember.
  • NLR Annual Technology Baseline, 2024 release: Attribution (CC BY). The 2024 release is CC BY 4.0; check each year's release.
  • OpenStreetMap roads and power lines: Share-alike or non-commercial. ODbL: credit OpenStreetMap and its contributors. A database built from it and shared publicly stays under ODbL; a map made from it carries the credit, and may oblige you to offer the changed data.
  • IEA World Energy Outlook 2025 free dataset: Share-alike or non-commercial. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 IGO: credit, no commercial use, share alike.
  • ENTSO-E Transparency Platform: Check each item. Open data policy since 2019, but check the terms of each item.
  • A co-op's meter data, shared by a co-author: Check each item. Governed by the data agreement, not an open licence: read it before you share anything (R9).

A check the rules do not pass: with the course's choices, 65 night hours (starting 00:00 to 03:00 local time) still show solar above 5% of its peak, 49 of them written by the same-hour fill. The log reports it as a known limit; a night rule is the next step (B2).

A spatial layer

Your study adds wind-turbine locations from the U.S. Wind Turbine Database for Prairie Town's county (illustrative). The file stores latitude and longitude in degrees (EPSG:4326).

  • Which coordinate system for 1 km setback buffers (the zone within 1 km of each turbine)? Right: a local projected system in metres, such as the county's UTM zone, keeps distances close to true.
  • And for the buildable area in square kilometres? Right: an equal-area system for the contiguous United States keeps areas true.

Two answers to the same task

Weak answer · Developing

Data from EIA [1]. We removed outliers [2] and filled missing values [3], and converted times to local time [4]. Annual demand was about 299 TWh [5].

  1. Falls short: Which form, area, files, download date and licence? A reader cannot find the same file.
  2. Falls short: No rule and no count; and removing is not flagging: the hours vanish from every total.
  3. Falls short: EIA's adjusted file has no blanks: the gaps are the hours you flagged. Which method, and how many hours?
  4. Falls short: Which zone, and what happened to the 23- and 25-hour days?
  5. Falls short: Raw or cleaned? Here both round to 299 TWh (299.48 and 298.56), so only a log can say which.

Strong answer · Proficient (audit trail)

Source: EIA Form EIA-930 six-month BALANCE files, balancing authority SWPP, 2025, accessed 2 November 2026, SHA-256 fac4bb99… and 1146158b… [1]. Extraction: EIA's adjusted columns; each fuel's columns summed, negative values set to 0 [2]. Licence: public domain, cited with the date [3]. Time: rows kept in UTC, hour ending; results labelled in Central time with daylight saving [4]. Spikes: demand under half the median (16,541 MW): 1 hour, 21 June, 1,505 MW [5], flagged as implausible; to check against SPP's records. Frozen: runs of 3 or more identical non-zero readings: demand 698 h, wind 671 h, solar 1,377 h [6]; suspected stuck feeds, as in the course's data build. Fill: the same UTC hour on valid days within 7, 21 or 45 days, to keep the daily shape. Effect: annual demand 298.56 TWh (raw 299.48); lowest hour 24.4 GW (raw 1.5) [7]; leaving the flagged hours out would give 275.09 TWh [8]. Known limits: 65 night hours still show solar above 5% of its peak, 49 of them filled; a night rule comes next [9]. Rerun: the starter notebook r04-data.ipynb, with these four settings, prints the four numbers [10]. Validation: not yet; studio R5 adds the verification log and a check against an independent source [11].

Defend, not just clean
For each step: the rule, the count, the reason, and what the result would be with the alternative.
Flag, then fill
Never delete silently: flag by a stated rule, then fill or leave out, and log which. A flag is a suspicion until other evidence confirms it.
Check the result
Run a check the rules did not make, such as solar at night, and log what still fails.
Time
Keep the rows as published (UTC, hour ending) and change only the labels.
Licences
Public domain still asks for a citation. Share-alike terms can differ for a database you derive and for a map made from it: check which apply to your output.
Go deeperWhy this page does not fill demand from the forecast

The course's own data build fills flagged demand hours from SPP's day-ahead forecast, rescaled to the demand around each gap, which gives a lowest hour of 23.7 GW.

This raw file carries no forecast column, so here every column uses the same-hour rule, which gives 24.4 GW.

Two defensible fills, two answers: that difference belongs in the note.

Go deeperWhat this studio leaves out
  • Metadata standards and data papers: the FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable) ask that machines, not only people, can find and read your data.
  • Personal data: meter readings, survey answers and addresses need consent and protection before any cleaning (R9).
  • Revisions: agencies revise past data; the access date and checksums in your note are what make a revision visible.
