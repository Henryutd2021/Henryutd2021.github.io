What you just did, as diagrams

Value of lost load
What an hour or a kWh without power costs someone; it differs by customer and by outage length.
Expected outage cost
Outages a year × their length × the cost of an hour, summed over outage lengths: $70,200 a year for the hospital here.
The next hour
Buy another hour of backup while it saves more a year than it costs; then check that the whole design covers its fixed costs.
Value stacking
Everyday savings can carry a design that outage savings alone cannot, if one battery can do both: here it is assumed full when each outage starts.
Who owns it
At a fixed borrowing rate, changing who pays moves the gains between parties, not the combined total. Here each owner also borrows at its own rate, so the net changes too.

Go deeper · AnalystSpreading a cost over the years

A sum paid now becomes a cost a year through the capital recovery factor, CRF:

\[\text{CRF} = \frac{r}{1-(1+r)^{-n}}\]

  • \(r\) is the borrowing rate a year (here 7%, or the owner’s) and \(n\) the number of years (15). At 7% over 15 years, CRF ≈ 0.1098: $740,000 of equipment costs about $81,248 a year.
  • K3 explains discounting; Analyst lesson A1 builds a project’s cash flows year by year.

  • This lesson tests one size at a time. A sizing model chooses battery, generator and solar together, hour by hour, and can treat the hospital’s survival as a rule rather than a cost.
  • A rule (“survive 72 hours”) and a cost (“an hour without power costs $9,000”) can give different designs: the second weighs the rare long outages by how rarely they come.

Check yourself, then practise

Three myths and one question, then a practice set of about 30 minutes.

Myth or fact? “A microgrid that keeps a hospital open always pays for itself.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. Here a 2-hour battery comes out at −$48,849 a year on outages alone, and at +$5,151 with the campus's bill savings, if it is full at every outage.

Myth or fact? “The value of lost load is one number.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. In the 2025 co-op survey, an 8-hour outage cost a household $3.22 and a business $41 per kWh not delivered.

Myth or fact? “If a microgrid saves money overall, its owner comes out ahead.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. The owner pays the capital and gains only through rates, a fee or the savings it keeps. Its borrowing rate moves the net too: +$3,682 a year at 10%, +$34,416 at 4%.

Outages of 4 hours hit the campus 0.4 times a year, and the hospital loses $9,000 an hour. What do they cost the hospital in a year, on average?

  • $3,600
  • $14,400
  • $36,000
Answer

$14,400: 0.4 outages × 4 hours × $9,000 an hour.

Practice · about 30 minutes on your own

Use the numbers in the steps above (2025 dollars; the campus's are illustrative).

  1. Expected outage hours. Outages of 1, 4, 12, 36 and 72 hours happen 0.8, 0.4, 0.15, 0.06 and 0.02 times a year. How many hours without power a year, on average?

    Answer

    7.8 hours: 0.8 × 1 + 0.4 × 4 + 0.15 × 12 + 0.06 × 36 + 0.02 × 72.

  2. The fifth hour of battery. At $9,000 an hour, what does a battery's fifth hour save a year? Does it pay at $14,975?

    Answer

    Outages longer than 4 hours happen 0.23 times a year, so it saves $2,070 a year: far below $14,975.

  3. When the first hour passes. At what cost of an hour without power would the battery's first hour save as much as it costs?

    Answer

    $14,975 ÷ 1.43 outages a year = $10,472 an hour. The fixed costs would still need covering.

  4. Value against price. A household's 24-hour outage costs $1.96 per kWh not delivered. How many times the 2025 U.S. residential average of 17.23¢ per kWh?

    Answer

    About 11 times.

  5. The cost of borrowing. The battery-and-generator design costs $740,000 to build, spread over 15 years. Its capital cost a year at 4% and at 10%?

    Answer

    $66,556 and $97,291: the same equipment, $30,734 a year apart.

  6. The mill's hours. Why does the mill at midday lower the village's cost per kWh, while the mill in the evening helps less?

    Answer

    At midday the panels run the mill directly: $0.31 falls to $0.23. In the evening the battery must grow from 40 to 70 kWh: $0.28.

In the real world

In the 2025 survey of rural co-op members, 65% of businesses said a three-day outage would shut them down, and 16% would keep going on backup generation (Berkeley Lab and partners, p. 11).

Beyond outages, on-site energy can also change where a business chooses to build:

Because the transactive energy market brought a new revenue stream, locations with stronger renewables became competitive to establish facilities despite higher fixed and logistics cost.

Contrary to intuition, energy storage systems, which balance intermittent power and uncertain load, had limited benefits for cost saving.

Approved excerpt of: Li et al. (2023), Applied Mathematical Modelling.
  • Holds when: stylized networks with synthetic distances and costs, candidate sites in California, Arizona and Nevada, and one year, planned week by week for products and hour by hour for energy.
  • Leaves out: grid outages, which the studies do not model.
