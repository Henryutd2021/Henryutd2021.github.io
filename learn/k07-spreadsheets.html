Units and assumptions (illustrative)
Item Value What it means
Units MW, MWh MW is power, the average over each hour; MWh is energy, MW held for one hour.
E1 100 MW The nuclear plant's steady output, as in lesson 0. Fixed in this sheet.
E2 $40/MWh Running cost of the gas combined-cycle plant. You change it in step 7.
Left out – How fast the gas plant can change its output (its ramp rate).

What you just did, as diagrams

Address
A column letter and a row number, such as B24; formulas point at addresses, not at numbers.
Relative address
B5 becomes B6 when filled down one row: right for data that changes row by row.
Absolute address
$E$1 never moves: right for one input, such as a price.
Range and function
SUM(F5:F28) adds every cell from F5 to F28; MAX, MIN and AVERAGE work the same way.
Check cell
A total computed two ways, which must agree: F30 against E30 × E2. Agreement shows the sheet is consistent, not that its inputs are right.
Go deeper · AnalystThe same column in Python

A spreadsheet column with one formula filled down is what a data table calls a vectorised operation: one line acts on every row at once. With the day in a pandas table df and the inputs as plain variables:

nuclear = 100                                              # E1: MW, runs flat
gas_cost = 40                                              # E2: $/MWh
df["gas_mw"] = df["demand"] - df["wind"] - df["solar"] - nuclear
df["gas_cost"] = df["gas_mw"] * 1 * gas_cost               # MW × 1 h × $/MWh
df["gas_cost"].sum(), df["gas_mw"].max()                   # F30 and E31

No addresses and no fill-down, so no sliding E1. Bridge B2 does this with a whole year of real hourly data.

Go deeper · ModelerWhen to leave the spreadsheet
  • Size: a year of hours is 8,760 rows per column; many plants or regions multiply that quickly.
  • Checking: a formula hidden in one cell is easy to break and hard to review; code can be read, tested and versioned (toolbox K8, upcoming).
  • Optimization: a sheet computes what a plan costs; choosing the best plan needs a solver (bridge B1).
  • Still useful: quick estimates, a check of a model's totals, and tables to share. Many studies keep a sheet for exactly that.

Check yourself

Myth or fact? “Typing 120 into E5 is as good as the formula, as long as the number is right.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. A typed number does not follow its inputs: change the demand or the nuclear output and E5 silently goes wrong.

Myth or fact? “=E5*$E$2 filled down to row 6 becomes =E6*$E$2.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Fact. E5 is relative and moves one row; $E$2 is absolute and stays.

A formula in B10 reads =B5*$A10. Filled right to C10, it becomes…

  • =B5*$A10
  • =C5*$A10
  • =C5*$B10
  • =B5*$B10
Answer

=C5*$A10: B5 moves one column; $A10 has its column fixed, so it stays in column A.

Each row is one hour. What unit does =SUM(E5:E28) have, if column E is in MW?

  • MW
  • MW per hour
  • MWh, because each row lasts 1 h
  • $
Answer

MWh: each row's MW lasts one hour, so each adds MW × 1 h. With 15-minute rows you would multiply the sum by 0.25 h.

Practice sheet

Use the table of the day above. With JavaScript on, you type each answer as a formula in a working sheet (about 30 minutes).

  1. Total demand energy over the day, in MWh (each row is one hour).
    Answer=SUM(B5:B28) = 6,957 MWh
  2. Share of the day's demand met by wind and solar, in %.
    Answer=SUM(C5:D28)/SUM(B5:B28)*100 = 37.3%
  3. Average gas need over the five hourly rows labelled 17:00 to 21:00, in MW.
    Answer=AVERAGE(E22:E26) = 112 MW
  4. The day's highest solar output, in MW.
    Answer=MAX(D5:D28) = 161 MW
  5. The day's gas cost if gas cost $55 per MWh, without changing E2.
    Answer=E30*55 = $108,075
