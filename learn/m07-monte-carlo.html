What could happen? Scenarios, Monte Carlo and global sensitivity

Sample five uncertain inputs with a Latin hypercube, run a delay-cost model 1,000 times and rank the inputs by influence

Modeler lesson M7 of Energy Systems, Step by Step: compare scenarios, a one-at-a-time tornado and stacked corners; sample five uncertain inputs of a waiting load’s delay cost with a Latin hypercube, run the model 1,000 times with a stated seed, read percentiles and chances with their Monte Carlo error, and rank the inputs by Spearman’s rank correlation; a Python notebook rebuilds it.

The developer's cost of a 24-month wait, 1,000 runs (illustrative)

  1. One number: at the base inputs, a 24-month wait costs the developer $171.1M in present value, and other customers $14.7M.
  2. Scenarios, with no chance attached: the developer's cost is $88.8M, $171.1M and $247.3M for waits of 12, 24 and 36 months.
  3. One at a time: the margin swings the developer's cost most, from $137.8M to $204.4M; capital and the discount rate swing it by $28.5M and $28.0M.
  4. Stacked corners: every input at its low-cost end gives $111.8M; at its high-cost end, $234.7M.
  5. Sampling 10 runs with seed 7: plain random leaves 4 of 10 margin slices and 3 of 10 discount-rate slices empty; a Latin hypercube leaves none.
  6. 1,000 Latin hypercube runs: median $170.3M; 80% of runs from $140.9M to $200.1M; the runs span $120.0M to $230.2M.
  7. Near a corner (margin, capital and discount rate all in the same outer tenth of their ranges): 2 runs; a chance of 0.1³ per corner predicts 2.
  8. Monte Carlo error over 20 seeds: P90 $200.1M ± $1.3M (two standard errors) with 1,000 runs, ± $4.7M with 100.
  9. A chance under these inputs: above $200M, 0.0% of runs at 12 months, 10.1% at 24 and 92.9% at 36.
  10. Spearman's rank correlation with the developer's cost: margin 0.86 (rank 1); then capital 0.38 and discount rate 0.31 (tied, rank 2–3); with other customers' cost: upgrades 0.81 (rank 1); then never arrives 0.54 (rank 2).
  11. Narrow the margin's range to ± $5,000: capital 0.64 and discount rate 0.59 (tied, rank 1–2), and the margin (0.47) ranks 3.

With JavaScript on, you run these studies yourself, step by step.

  • Toy: the waiting load of case file CF7, 100 MW: illustrative round numbers in one price level (no inflation). Other customers pay the utility's costs (no large-load tariff); no bridge power.
  • Ranges: invented around CF7's values, each uniform and drawn independently; the wait is a scenario of 12, 24 or 36 months.
  • Accounts: present values, each run at its own discount rate, month by month; the two accounts are never added.
  • Sampling: Latin hypercube, seed 7, generator mulberry32; percentiles by linear interpolation, as in K5; Monte Carlo error from 20 repeats with seeds 1 to 20.

About 35 guided minutes in ten steps. Every run is computed in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Go deeper · ModelerThe formulas: the toy, the Latin hypercube, ρ and the Monte Carlo error
  • The toy. With \(i = (1+r)^{1/12} - 1\) and \(A = \frac{1 - (1+i)^{-\tau}}{i}\), the developer’s cost is \(D = (m P + K i)\,A\) and other customers’ is \(R = U i A + \min(0.5,\, h\tau)\, U (1+i)^{-\tau}\).
  • Symbols. \(m\) margin ($ per MW a month), \(P\) = 100 MW, \(K\) idle capital ($), \(r\) yearly discount rate, \(U\) upgrade cost ($), \(\tau\) the wait (months).
  • The never-arrives chance. \(h\) is in percentage points added a month, not compounded: \(h\tau\) = 24 × 0.5 points = 12%, capped at 50%.
  • Latin hypercube. Run \(a\)’s draw of input \(j\) is \(u_{a,j} = (\pi_j(a) + \xi_{a,j})/N\), with \(\pi_j\) a random ordering of \(0, \dots, N-1\) and \(\xi\) uniform on \([0, 1)\); the value is \(\text{lo} + u\,(\text{hi} - \text{lo})\).
  • Spearman’s ρ. The correlation of the ranks; with no ties, \(\rho = 1 - \frac{6 \sum_a d_a^2}{N (N^2 - 1)}\), where \(d_a\) is run \(a\)’s rank of the input minus its rank of the cost.
  • Monte Carlo error. The standard error of an estimate is the standard deviation of that estimate over 20 repeats with other seeds; 20 repeats make the error itself rough, about ± 16%.
  • What the hypercube buys. Over 20 seeds, the mean cost of 1,000 runs scatters by $0.03M with a Latin hypercube and $0.80M with plain random draws (one standard error); the P90, by $0.65M and $0.80M.
  • A share’s error. For plain random draws, K5’s \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/N}\) gives 0.95 points for the 10.1% above $200M; the Latin hypercube repeats scatter by 0.52 points.
  • Near a corner. With continuous inputs, an exact corner has zero chance. Near one (the margin, capital and discount rate all in the same outer tenth of their ranges) has 0.1³ = 0.1% per corner, so 1,000 runs expect 2; seed 7 has 2, both at the high-cost end.
Go deeper · ModelerWhat a rank correlation misses, and what comes next
  • Monotone effects only. ρ reads how consistently the cost rises or falls with an input. An input that matters only through another (an interaction) or pushes the cost up and then down can show ρ near 0.
  • Variance shares. Sobol indices split the variance of the cost among the inputs and their interactions; with \(k\) inputs they need about \(N(k+2)\) runs. The lab estimates them for this toy.
  • Inputs that move together. Draw them with a stated rank correlation (Iman and Conover’s method; the lab’s practice task 3 uses a simplified two-input version), or sample whole records from data, as K5 suggests.
  • Slow models. When 1,000 runs take too long, a fast stand-in fitted to a few runs does the sampling (M9); deciding before the uncertainty resolves is M8.

Check yourself, then try it

Myth or fact? “A P90 of $200.1M means a 10% chance that the real wait costs more.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. 10% of runs cost more, under the assumed ranges, uniform shapes and independent inputs. Change any of those and the P90 moves.

Myth or fact? “With 1,000 runs, a Monte Carlo answer is exact.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. Over 20 seeds, the P90 of 1,000 runs scatters by ± $1.3M (2 standard errors). More runs shrink that; no run count fixes a wrong range.

Myth or fact? “The input with the largest rank correlation matters most in the real world.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. ρ ranks inputs within the ranges you assumed: narrow the margin's to ± $5,000 and it drops from first to third.

50 of 1,000 random runs exceed a budget. The share, with its standard error from K5's formula?

  • 5.0% ± 0.7 points
  • 5.0% ± 5.0 points
  • 50% ± 1.6 points
  • 5.0%, exactly
Answer

5.0% ± 0.7 points: √(0.05 × 0.95 ÷ 1,000) = 0.0069, about 0.7 percentage points.

A report says only “P90 cost: $200M”. What should it add first?

  • More decimal places
  • The cost at the base inputs
  • Ranges and shapes, run count, seed and scatter
  • The name of the computer it ran on
Answer

The inputs, the run count, the seed and the scatter: without them nobody can judge or repeat the P90.

Where this came up in research

A present-value framework turns the delayed computing capacity into the grid MW it needs and prices the delay in three ledgers.

Inputs are screened with Latin hypercube sampling.

The delay is treated as exogenous and parameters are illustrative; network feasibility is not modeled, and the social perspective is illustrated with a single bridge-power example.
