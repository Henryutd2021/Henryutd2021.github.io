  • Useful energy. Official statistics stop at final energy. For the real countries, useful energy here is a what-if: their final energy by sector at Prairieland's sector efficiencies (illustrative), not a measurement.
  • Efficiencies. Prairieland's sector efficiencies are its useful energy ÷ its final energy, sector by sector, from its own fuel mix: illustrative averages, not measurements.
  • Final energy. Final energy here leaves out non-energy use (feedstocks); the energy industry's own use sits before the meter unless the country's note says otherwise.
  • United States. U.S. final energy is EIA's end-use energy, which keeps some of the energy industry's own use: natural gas lease and plant fuel in industry, pipeline fuel in transport.

  • Physical energy content. Nuclear counts as its reactors' heat (measured, or electricity ÷ 0.33 if unknown); hydro, wind and solar as the electricity they make.
  • Substitution. Every MWh from nuclear, hydro, wind and solar counts as the fuel a thermal plant would burn to make it.
  • Direct equivalent. Every MWh from nuclear, hydro, wind and solar counts at its electricity, 3.6 GJ.
  • China. China publishes 17.9% for primary electricity and other energy, in coal equivalent, a kind of substitution; by physical energy content its nuclear, hydro, wind and solar come to about 9.6% (the page's estimate).
  • Japan. Japan's METI counts each kWh from nuclear, hydro, solar, wind and geothermal as 8.49 MJ, the fuel of a thermal plant at 42.4%: a kind of substitution.
  • Japan, converted. Converted to physical energy content, Japan's primary energy is 16.9 EJ, not METI's 17.5 EJ; renewables 7.8%, not 12.0%.

Three more lenses: resources, climate and institutions

  • Resources: what the country produces at home: rivers, coal, oil, gas, crops.
  • Climate: buildings' share of final energy: heating and cooling raise it, and so do bigger homes, higher incomes and how statistics group services; the table cannot tell these apart.
  • Institutions: who runs the power system and how electricity is traded, from the regional cards.

Go deeper · AnalystWhy the substitution method enlarges electricity
  • The rule. A thermal plant at efficiency η burns 3.6 ÷ η PJ of fuel for each TWh it makes. Substitution counts every TWh from nuclear, hydro, wind and solar that way.
  • The factor. 2.5 times its electricity at 40%, about 3 times at 33%.
  • Nuclear under physical content. Its reactors' heat: measured where it is reported (the EU), from the plants' average heat rate (the U.S.), or electricity ÷ 0.33 where the heat is not known (India, Brazil, this page's China and Japan figures).
  • Recompute the share. Substitution changes nuclear's assigned energy (electricity ÷ 0.40) and enlarges hydro, wind and solar, so the total changes too: the United States' nuclear is 8.5% by physical content, 6.7% by substitution.
  • Direct equivalent counts every TWh at 3.6 PJ, so nuclear's energy falls to about a third of its physical-content value.
  • China's coal equivalent is a substitution at the year's coal burned per kWh in thermal plants.
  • Japan's balance is a substitution at 8.49 MJ per kWh, a thermal plant at 42.4%.

Units as published

  • Quad: a quadrillion (10^15) Btu; 1 quad = 1.05506 EJ. Used by the United States.
  • TJ: a terajoule, a thousandth of a PJ. Used by the EU and Japan.
  • tce: a metric ton of coal equivalent, 29.3076 GJ. China counts in units of 10,000 tce.
  • ktoe: a thousand metric tons of oil equivalent; 1 toe = 41.868 GJ. Used by India.
  • Mtep: a million tep, Brazil's ton of oil equivalent, 10⁷ kcal = 41.868 GJ.
  • TWh: a terawatt-hour, 3.6 PJ. Used by Norway.

Check yourself

Practice, about 10 minutes: three myths, then four questions.

Myth or fact? “A country's renewable share of primary energy is a single, fixed number.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. Norway's is 48% by physical energy content and 68% by substitution, for the same plants in the same year.

Myth or fact? “A country that produces more energy than it uses imports none.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. Norway produced 2,593 TWh in 2025 and still imported 124 TWh, while exporting 2,380 TWh.

Myth or fact? “If every country used energy at Prairieland's sector efficiencies, most of its primary energy would end up as useful heat, motion and light.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth, in this what-if: for the six real countries drawn here, 43% to 48% of primary energy would become useful energy. Their real efficiencies differ and are not in official statistics.

Which of the eight gets 60% of its primary energy from coal and lignite and imports 40% of its supply?

  • India
  • The European Union
  • Brazil
Answer

India (fiscal year 2023-24): coal and lignite 60% of the sources its balance covers (traditional biomass is not in the balance); net import dependency 40.27%, as MoSPI publishes it.

By substitution at 40% efficiency, 1 TWh of wind electricity (3.6 PJ) counts as how many PJ of primary energy?

  • 3.6 PJ
  • 9 PJ
  • 10.9 PJ
Answer

3.6 ÷ 0.40 = 9.0 PJ: the fuel a thermal plant at 40% would burn to make it, 2.5 times the electricity.

The EU's import dependency was 57.3% in 2024. What does Eurostat divide by what?

  • Net imports ÷ gross available energy
  • Imports ÷ production
  • Production ÷ gross available energy
Answer

Net imports (imports less exports) ÷ gross available energy, the EU's primary energy.

China publishes 17.9% for primary electricity and other energy, in coal equivalent. By physical energy content, nuclear, hydro, wind and solar come to about…

  • the same
  • a little more
  • about half as much
Answer

About 9.6% (the page's estimate): coal equivalent counts each kWh as the coal a thermal plant would burn, so NBS's figure is 2.6 times the four sources' electricity (it also holds some other energy).
