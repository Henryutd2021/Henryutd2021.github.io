Researcher checkpoint: defend your question and method, then revise

About four hours: review a stranger’s study, get a written review of your own, answer it, revise, and rerun your main result from a clean clone

The Researcher checkpoint (CK-R) of Energy Systems, Step by Step: calibrate on a fictional co-op study’s proposal, then defend your own research capstone’s question and method in a written mock review, answer each concern, revise and log the changes, and rerun your main result from a clean clone. An annotated review, response and revision open when you submit. Answers and times stay in your browser.

Task

How this checkpoint works · about 240 minutes, no hints

  1. A · A stranger's study (about 45 min). Review the co-op proposal below with the review form and submit it as task 1; the seven marked tasks (2–5, 9–11) open after it.
  2. B · Your mock review (about 60 min). Get a written review of your research capstone's question and method: from a mentor, a peer, or yourself after a day away.
  3. C · Respond and revise (about 70 min). Answer each concern point by point; revise your proposal and log each change.
  4. D · Rerun (about 50 min). Order and check the co-op study's rerun (tasks 9 to 11), then rerun your own main result from a clean clone.
  5. Submit (about 15 min). Enter your results in the twelve tasks below, in any order. The worked review, response and revision open once all twelve are answered.

When to take it: once your research capstone has a proposal and a main result. A review from someone else can take days: ask for it first, and do part A while you wait.

How it is marked, before you start

  • Proficient: at least 5 of the 7 auto-marked tasks right on the first try, plus at least 3 of the 4 points of each written task (1, 6, 7, 8 and 12).
  • Strong: at least 6 of 7 on the first try, and every written point.
  • Opening solutions early: a task whose answer you open first counts as not right (a written task: no points). Opening the worked solution before all twelve are answered makes the attempt practice, with no level.
  • After the worked solution opens: an answer given after that counts as practice, not as right, and a written task then earns no points.
  • Task 1 first: the seven marked tasks open once your review is submitted. Each written task stays as you submit it. A review submitted after a marked task was answered earns no points.
  • The level is not the table's: it comes from this rule. The criterion table in the rubric section rates the work itself; its Strong asks for a review and a rerun by someone else.

Refreshers: R1 (framing), R3 (method), R5 (reruns), R8 (findings with limits), R9 (integrity), R10 (the question card), M14 (difference-in-differences), K8 (clones).

Words in the tasks: co-op (an electric cooperative), repository, clean clone, regression and agent-based model. Tap one for its meaning.

Part A · the proposal to review

Did a night-pumping tariff move irrigation out of the co-op's peak?

A graduate student's proposal, with a first result, for the board of Prairie Valley Co-op.

Question
Did Prairie Valley Co-op's 2024 night-pumping tariff lower irrigation peak demand?
Type
Evaluative.
Audience
The co-op's board, which votes in 2026 on keeping the tariff.
Billing
The co-op's wholesale bill charges for its load in its 20 highest-load summer hours.
The tariff
Cheaper energy from 22:00 to 06:00 and a higher price from 14:00 to 20:00 on summer weekdays. Each farm chose whether to join.
Data
Hourly meter readings of the co-op's 112 irrigation pumps, summers 2020 to 2024.
Metric
Peak demand, kW per pump.
Method
Difference-in-differences: the 41 pumps that joined in 2024 against the 71 that did not, before and after.
Literature
Five studies of time-of-use tariffs for households.
Checks
Run once, on data the author cleaned by hand in a spreadsheet.
First result
Peak demand fell 6.2 kW per pump.

The co-op, its farms and every number here are fictional: the results are assumed example outputs, not computed from data.

The forms

Four plain templates: one for a review, one for a response, a change log and a rerun report. Use them for the co-op proposal and for your own study.

The review form (parts A and B)
1. The study in one or two sentences: question, method, data.
2. The main claim it will make, and the evidence it plans.
3. Major concerns (at most three): ones that could change the conclusion. For each: the rubric competence, and what evidence or change would answer it.
4. Minor concerns.
5. One alternative method from another family, and what it would show that this one cannot.
6. Can the main result be rerun from the repository? What is missing?
7. Recommendation: proceed; proceed once the major concerns are answered; or rethink the question.
The response (part C)
For each concern, in the reviewer's order:
Concern 1 (competence): accepted / partly accepted / declined.
Reason: one or two sentences.
Change: what changed, and where (a section of the proposal, a file in the repository).
Evidence: a test, a figure or a number, where you have one.
The change log (part C)
One line per change:
Before → after · the concern it answers · where.
Then: your rubric self-rating after the revision, competence by competence, and any level that moved, with its evidence.
The rerun report (part D)
Repository and commit; date; machine and environment.
Tolerance for the main result, written down before the rerun.
Reported value; rerun value; difference as a share of the reported value (in the result's units if that value is near zero).
Qualitative work: the audit trail followed from the raw material, each theme traced to its sources, coding decisions and discrepancies explained.
Cause of any difference, and the fix in the repository.
Who reran it (you, or someone else).

Words used in the tasks and forms: clean clone, lock file, checksum, 95% interval, commit, environment, audit trail, matched comparison.

Reviewing a peer's proposal: keep it confidential and do not use its ideas without permission (ALLEA 2023, §2.8). Reviewing your own: wait a day, and read it as someone who has not seen it.

Without JavaScript: nothing here is locked or marked, and the worked solution is further down the page, so use this list as practice. A level needs the interactive tasks.

Two reviews of the co-op proposal

Review the co-op proposal: the study, the major and minor concerns, and what would answer each.

A weak review · Developing

Summary: An interesting study of a new tariff. Concerns: The literature review is thin. [1] The method seems fine. [2] Figure 2 is hard to read. [3] Recommendation: accept with minor changes. [4]

  1. Falls short: A minor concern led with as if it were the main one; no competence named, and nothing that would answer it.
  2. Falls short: Each farm chose whether to join: the comparison is untested.
  3. Falls short: Format, not substance: it cannot change the conclusion.
  4. Falls short: A verdict without reasons the authors can act on.

The worked review · Strong

Summary: asks whether the 2024 tariff lowered the pumps' peak demand, by difference-in-differences on 112 pumps' meter readings, 2020 to 2024. [1] Major 1 · Method fit: farms chose whether to join, so their demand may have moved differently even without the tariff; show the summers before, and try farms that joined later as a comparison. [2] Major 2 · Framing: say whose peak, in kW, over which hours: the co-op is billed for its 20 highest-load summer hours, not for each pump's own peak. [3] Major 3 · Trustworthy analysis: the hand cleaning in a spreadsheet is not documented, and the proposal does not supply what a rerun from the raw data needs. [4] Minor · Positioning: the five studies are of households; add studies of farm or business tariffs. [5] Another family: a pump-scheduling simulation would show how much pumping could move at most, which meter data cannot. [6] Recommendation: proceed once the three major concerns are answered. [7]

  1. Works: The study stated fairly, before any criticism.
  2. Works: The concern that could change the conclusion comes first, with the evidence that would answer it.
  3. Works: Tied to a competence, and to what the decision-maker needs.
  4. Works: Parts can still be checked independently; a full rerun from the raw data is not yet possible.
  5. Works: Kept minor: it could sharpen the method, but threatens the conclusion less than selection.
  6. Works: An alternative from another method family, and what it adds.
  7. Works: A verdict tied to the concerns.

Two answers to Major 1

Answer the concern that the farms chose whether to join.

A deflecting answer · Developing

Response: We thank the reviewer. Self-selection is a well-known limitation of observational studies, [1] and we have added it to the limitations section. [2]

  1. Falls short: It names the problem and keeps it.
  2. Falls short: A sentence moved; the analysis unchanged.

An answering response · Strong

Accepted, with thanks to the reviewer. Change: the comparison group is now the 29 farms that joined in 2025, before they joined (section 3; analysis/did.py). [1] Evidence: in the four summers before 2024, the two groups' peak-hour demand moved within 0.5 kW of each other each year (Figure 2). [2] Result: with this comparison and the metric of Major 2, the fall is 4.6 kW per pump, not 6.2. [3] Limit kept: the later joiners' demand might still have moved differently; stated in section 6. [4]

  1. Works: The change, and where it is made.
  2. Works: New evidence for the parallel-trends assumption.
  3. Works: The change moved the answer, and the response says so.
  4. Works: What the change cannot fix, said plainly.

The revision: what moved it from Proficient to Strong

The revised study's question, method, checks and result.

Revised: Proficient · Proficient

Question: Did the 2024 tariff lower the joining pumps' average demand, in kW per pump, in the co-op's 20 billing hours, compared with farms that joined in 2025, before they joined? [1] Would count against it: no larger fall among the joiners than among the 2025 joiners, or a gap between the groups that opened before 2024. [2] Method: difference-in-differences, defended against a matched comparison and a pump-scheduling simulation. [3] Checks: reruns from a clean clone within 1%; a verification log; the pumps' summed readings checked against the meters of the feeders that serve only pumps, same hours, after line losses. [4] Result: 4.6 kW per pump (95% interval 2.9 to 6.3).

  1. Proficient: Typed, with an audience (the board), a metric with units (the billing hours' average, not the co-op's single highest hour) and a counterfactual.
  2. Proficient: The falsification statement: the result that would show the tariff did not cause the fall.
  3. Proficient: Two alternatives, one from another family (simulation).
  4. Proficient: A clean-clone rerun, a log and one check against an independent reference (feeder meters): of the measurements, not of the tariff's effect.

Revised: Strong · Strong

Question: the same, and is the fall above 1.5 kW per pump, the least for which the board would keep the tariff? [1] Method: the same. Limit: the later joiners' demand might still have moved differently. Mitigated: in the four summers before 2024 the two groups moved together, within 0.5 kW a year. [2] Rerun: a co-op engineer reran the clean clone and got 4.6 kW. [3] Validation, set up first: summer 2025, not used to build the analysis: the same code on the 29 farms that joined in 2025, against the 42 that never joined. [4] Rule and result: the check supports the same decision for the 2025 cohort if its whole 95% interval is above 1.5 kW. 2025: 4.1 kW (1.8 to 6.4): it does. [5] Result: 4.6 kW per pump (95% interval 2.9 to 6.3), above 1.5 kW across the interval.

  1. Moves it to Strong: Framing's Strong level: the answer that would change the decision.
  2. Moves it to Strong: Method fit's Strong level: the method's limit stated and partly mitigated.
  3. Moves it to Strong: Trustworthy analysis at Strong, first half: reproduced by someone else.
  4. Moves it to Strong: Second half: data not used to build it, a named comparison group, and a rule written down before looking.
  5. Moves it to Strong: It supports the decision threshold, not the size of 4.6 kW, and is not proof the effects are equal: the estimates share farms, so no interval for their gap is given. The never-joiners, a new comparison group, need their own case.

The rerun, worked

Tolerance
1% of the main result, written down before the rerun.
Values
Reported 4.6 kW; rerun 4.3 kW. Difference: (4.6 − 4.3) ÷ 4.6 = 0.3 ÷ 4.6 = 0.065, or 6.5%: over the tolerance.
Cause
Three pumps whose meters were replaced mid-summer were removed by hand before the first run; the code kept them. The data note had set that rule before any result.
Fix
The rule, not agreement with the old number, decides: it is now in the cleaning script, and the rerun from a clean clone gives 4.6 kW.
Logged
Both numbers, the cause and the fix, in the verification log. Then a co-op engineer reran it from the repository: 4.6 kW.

Rubric

Your work, criterion by criterion

Criterion Not yet Developing Proficient Strong
The review No review. Comments on wording or format, or concerns with no competence and nothing that would answer them. Major concerns (those that could change the conclusion), each tied to a competence and to the evidence that would answer it; the method tested against another family. Also written by someone else, and ranked by what each concern could change in the conclusion.
The response No response. Thanks and promises with no change, or concerns declined without reasons. Every concern answered with a reason; each accepted one names its change and where; a concern about the analysis answered in the analysis. Also new evidence for the changes (a test, a figure), and the reviewer credited.
The revision No revision. Wording changed, the analysis not. A before → after log; the question still passes the card's eight checks; the rubric self-rating redone. Also each competence that moved up shown with its evidence, or, where none could, why.
The rerun No rerun. Rerun in the working folder, not a clean clone, or a difference not reported. A clean clone (or a complete audit trail), a tolerance set first, both values and the difference reported, any cause fixed. Also rerun by someone else from the repository, within the tolerance.

The checkpoint's level (not the table's)

  • Proficient: at least 5 of the 7 auto-marked tasks right on the first try, and at least 3 of the 4 points of each of the five written tasks.
  • Strong: at least 6 of 7 on the first try, and every point of all five written tasks.
  • Developing: at least 3 of 7 on the first try.
  • Not yet: anything else.
  • Solutions opened early: a task whose answer you open before answering counts as not right. If the worked solution opens before all twelve are answered, the attempt is practice, with no level.
  • Answered late: an answer given after the worked solution opened counts as practice, not as right; a written task submitted after it earns no points.
  • Task 1 first: a review submitted after a marked task was answered earns no points.
  • Not the table's Strong: this level needs no second person. The criterion table's Strong (a review and a rerun by someone else) is rated apart, by you or a mentor.

Self-rated: the written tasks, against their points. A mentor can rate them again with the table, from the copied summary.

Evidence, not a certificate: with your research capstone and its self-check, this checkpoint is evidence you can show.
