What you just did, as diagrams

Power and energy
A 25 MW, 100 MWh battery charges or discharges at up to 25 MW and holds up to 100 MWh: four hours flat out.
Round-trip efficiency
This battery returns 80% of what goes in; the rest becomes heat.
State of charge
The energy stored at the end of each hour; it links each hour to the next.
Value
The battery's value is what it saves the town: $7,240 on this day with the best schedule.
Decisions
For each hour: how much the battery charges and discharges, and how much it then holds.
Goal
The lowest running cost for the town's day.
Rules
At most 25 MW in or out; 0 to 100 MWh stored; 80% of what goes in is stored; supply equals demand every hour.

Go deeper · AnalystPrices, losses and the break-even spread
  • Break-even. A MWh stored at price \(p_\text{in}\) comes back as 0.8 MWh, so it pays only if the selling price beats \(p_\text{in}/0.8\): buy at $40, sell above $50.
  • Spilled sun. It costs $0, so storing it always passes that test.
  • One more MWh. Each hour’s price is the cost of one more MWh. A 25 MW battery is more than that: at 08:00 only 3 MW of sun is spilled, so full-power charging takes 22 MW from gas at $40.
  • The real saving. So this lesson counts what the town actually saves. Your best full-power schedule saves $6,360; the solver saves $7,240 by charging at part power, matching each hour’s spill.

Decisions for each hour \(t\): charging, discharging and the state of charge, with the gas plants and spill as in lesson 0.

Symbol Meaning Unit
\(t\) the hour, 0 to 23 (1 MW for one hour = 1 MWh) –
\(p_{\mathrm{cc},t}\), \(p_{\mathrm{pk},t}\) combined-cycle and peaker output MW
\(s_t\) spilled wind and sun MW
\(c_t\), \(d_t\) battery charging and discharging MW
\(e_t\) energy stored at the end of hour \(t\) MWh
\(g_t\) the gap: demand minus nuclear, wind and sun MW
\(w_t\), \(\sigma_t\) wind and sun output MW

\[\min \sum_t \big(40\,p_{\mathrm{cc},t} + 100\,p_{\mathrm{pk},t}\big)\]

Rules for every hour, top to bottom: balance, state of charge, power, energy, the gas plants’ sizes and spill.

\[ \begin{gathered} p_{\mathrm{cc},t} + p_{\mathrm{pk},t} - s_t + d_t - c_t = g_t\\ e_t = e_{t-1} + 0.8\,c_t - d_t, \quad e_{-1} = 0\\ 0 \le c_t,\ d_t \le 25\\ 0 \le e_t \le 100\\ 0 \le p_{\mathrm{cc},t} \le 150, \quad 0 \le p_{\mathrm{pk},t} \le 100\\ 0 \le s_t \le w_t + \sigma_t \end{gathered} \]

  • Over time. The state-of-charge rule is the only one linking one hour to the next; it makes storage a problem over time.
  • Losses. Here the whole loss sits on charging; many models split it, \(\sqrt{0.8}\) each way.
  • Left out of the goal. The nuclear plant’s $24,000 for the day is the same in every plan.
  • Always an answer. The page’s code also allows unserved demand at $3,000/MWh, so the model always has a solution; on this day none is needed.
  • Next. Lesson 10 writes such a model down and lesson 11 checks a solver’s answer. M3 (capacity expansion) chooses the battery’s size too, and A8 (how long should storage last?, upcoming) will.
Go deeperWhat this lesson leaves out
  • One day only. A battery's yearly value needs every day of the year, including days with no spilled sun (lesson A8, upcoming).
  • Perfect foresight: the solver knows the whole day in advance. Real operators schedule with forecasts that miss (lesson 4).
  • The cost of building the battery and of its wear, which would set a minimum spread worth cycling for (lesson 7).
  • The combined-cycle's ramp limit from lesson 0, and any limit on the wires.

Check yourself

Myth or fact? “A battery's MWh rating tells you its maximum power.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. The MWh say how much it holds; a separate MW rating says how fast. Prairie Town's 100 MWh battery delivers at most 25 MW, for four hours.

Myth or fact? “A battery adds energy to the grid.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. It moves energy in time and loses some on the way: this one returns 80% of what goes in.

Myth or fact? “Buying at $40/MWh and selling at $45/MWh pays for this battery.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. 1 MWh bought for $40 returns 0.8 MWh, worth $36 at $45. It needs a selling price above $50.

A 50 MW, 200 MWh battery with 80% round trip charges 100 MWh at $20/MWh and later sells all it stored at $60/MWh. What does it earn?

  • $4,000
  • $2,800
  • $4,800
  • $2,000
Answer

$2,800: it stores 80 MWh and sells them for 80 × $60 = $4,800, minus the $2,000 it paid.

On 9 June 2025, going from a 4-hour to an 8-hour battery (both 25 MW) raises Prairie Town's saving by how much?

  • It doubles
  • About half again
  • Only $96
  • Nothing at all
Answer

Only $96 ($7,240 to $7,336): it stores the last 3 MWh of spilled sun and returns 2.4 MWh at $40. The peaker's last 5 MWh remain, since both discharge at most 25 MW.
