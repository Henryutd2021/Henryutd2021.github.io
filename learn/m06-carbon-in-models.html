Putting carbon in the model: caps, prices, frontiers and procurement rules

Cap the Plains Grid’s CO₂ in a planning LP, trace the frontier, compare a carbon price, then add an hourly-matching rule and see what it costs and what it changes

Illustrative grid. The Plains Grid of A10 and A12, fictional, with EIA's 2025 gas heat rates, gas price and CO₂ coefficient.

Costs. New wind and solar at EIA's AEO2026 values; a 4-hour battery at NREL's 2025 update, moved to 2025 dollars; 4% real.

Six days for a year. Each season's most typical day of 2025, plus the calmest day and the day of the highest net load, weighted by the days they stand for (EIA-930 shapes).

About 40 guided minutes in seven steps. Solver: HiGHS (open source), loaded on your first solve (about 3.5 MB, once).

Build it in Python · notebook lab, about 45 minutes, then 90 minutes of practice

The same planning LP in scipy.optimize.linprog(method="highs"): caps and their duals, the frontier, prices and their ties, the two matching rules, and the full-year checks. assert cells check your numbers against the lesson's reference block.

Go deeper · ModelerThe planning LP in algebra

For each hour \(t\) of the six days, with weight \(w_t\) (the days it stands for); each step lasts one hour, so \(x\) MW for an hour is \(x\) MWh:

\[\min\; \sum_{k} C_k X_k + \sum_t w_t \big( c_{cc}\,\mathit{cc}_t + c_{ct}\,\mathit{ct}_t + V\,u_t \big)\]

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathit{we}_t + \mathit{se}_t + \mathit{wn}_t + \mathit{sn}_t + \mathit{cc}_t + \mathit{ct}_t + d_t - c_t + u_t &= D_t && \text{(balance)}\\ \mathit{wn}_t \le f^w_t\, W, \quad \mathit{sn}_t \le f^s_t\, S, \quad c_t, d_t \le B, \quad e_t &\le 4B && \text{(sizes)}\\ \mathit{we}_t \le \bar w_t, \quad \mathit{se}_t \le \bar s_t, \quad \mathit{cc}_t \le 2{,}000, \quad \mathit{ct}_t &\le 2{,}300 && \text{(what exists)}\\ W, S \le 8{,}000, \quad B &\le 4{,}000 && \text{(build limits)}\\ e_t = e_{t-1} + 0.85\, c_t - d_t &&& \text{(battery; each day ends with the energy it began with)}\\ \sum_t w_t \big( r_{cc}\,\mathit{cc}_t + r_{ct}\,\mathit{ct}_t \big) &\le \varepsilon && \text{(the cap)} \end{aligned} \]

Every variable is zero or more.

Symbol Meaning Unit
\(X_k\), \(C_k\) new wind \(W\), solar \(S\), battery \(B\); each one’s yearly capital and upkeep MW; $ per MW a year
\(\mathit{we}, \mathit{se}, \mathit{wn}, \mathit{sn}\) existing and new wind and solar used MW
\(\bar w_t\), \(\bar s_t\) what the existing wind and solar could make that hour MW
\(\mathit{cc}\), \(\mathit{ct}\) output of the gas combined-cycle plants and of the gas peakers (combustion turbines) MW
\(c_{cc}\), \(c_{ct}\); \(r_{cc}\), \(r_{ct}\) their running cost and CO₂ per MWh $/MWh; t/MWh
\(c_t, d_t, e_t\) battery charge, discharge and energy MW; MW; MWh
\(u_t\) unserved demand MW
\(V\) the value of lost load, $10,000 $/MWh
\(f^w_t\), \(f^s_t\), \(D_t\) capacity factors per MW, and demand share; MW
\(\varepsilon\) the cap t a year
  • A price \(\tau\) drops the cap and adds \(\tau \sum_t w_t (r_{cc}\,\mathit{cc}_t + r_{ct}\,\mathit{ct}_t)\) to the goal.
  • Hourly matching adds the buyer’s own \(W_b, S_b, B_b\) and, every hour, \(\mathit{wb}_t + \mathit{sb}_t + d^b_t - c^b_t \ge 300\), with \(c^b_t \le \mathit{wb}_t + \mathit{sb}_t\).
  • Annual matching keeps one row: \(\sum_t w_t (\mathit{wb}_t + \mathit{sb}_t) \ge 300 \times 8{,}760\).
Go deeper · ModelerCaps, prices and ties
  • One dual, checked by finite steps. The test loosens and tightens each cap by 1,000 t; the cost changes at the dual’s rate both ways. That is a sensitivity check, not a proof.
  • A positive dual means the cap binds in every best plan. This is complementary slackness: a rule with a nonzero dual holds exactly. So the cap’s CO₂ is the same for all of them, though their schedules may differ.
  • A price ties a stretch. Where the frontier is straight, a price equal to the magnitude of its downward slope makes every plan on that stretch score the same; the solver returns one of them.
  • So report the range. The page solves twice more at each price, for the least and the most CO₂ among the best plans, and shows the range when they differ.
Go deeper · ModelerSix days against the full year
  • Annual matching. On all 8,760 hours of 2025 the 844 MW of wind make 95% of the data center's load over the year: the six weighted days were windier than the year.
  • Hourly matching. Run hour by hour through 2025, the portfolio leaves at least 290 MWh unmatched, 0.01% of the load's year, in hours of low wind and sun. Which hours carry it can differ between equally good schedules.
  • So: a rule met on representative days is a representative-day result. Check it on the full year, as the notebook does.
Go deeperWhat this model leaves out
  • Demand that answers to prices, and the other years' weather.
  • Lines inside the grid: every plant serves every load.
  • Retiring the gas plants, and their fixed costs.
  • Uncertainty: the plan knows the six days in advance (M7 samples what could happen).

Check yourself

Myth or fact? “A carbon price equal to a cap's dual gives back exactly the cap's plan.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. At the 70% cap's dual ($22.81 per t, rounded; the tie needs its full precision), every plan from 3.15 Mt to 3.64 Mt scores the same. The cap's plan is one of them.

Myth or fact? “If a buyer's purchases let it claim zero emissions, the plants emit no more than without it.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. With certificates from existing wind the data center claims 0 t, and the plants emit +1.14 Mt a year more.

Myth or fact? “In an LP, the cost of cutting one more tonne never falls as the cap tightens.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Fact. The LP's frontier is convex: here the dual rises from $21.98 at 90% to $130.61 at 30%.

A cap's dual is $22.81 per t. Loosening the cap by 1,000 t changes the best cost by about how much?

  • It rises by about $22,810
  • It falls by about $22,810
  • It falls by $22.81
  • Nothing: the dual is only a price
Answer

It falls by about $22,810: $22.81 × 1,000 t, the dual rounded to the cent, while the cap stays in the dual's range.

In the real world

Reductions are computed under average-emission-intensity accounting with perfect foresight; they are not estimates of the consequential (marginal) system response.

From: Li et al. (2025), Cell Reports Sustainability.

Case file CF6: how much shifting load to cleaner hours can cut emissions, and when.

A two-stage stochastic linear program sized electrolyzers, hydrogen tanks and fuel cells once in each of ERCOT's eight weather zones, with hydrogen pipelines between neighbouring zones, then scheduled the system hour by hour over a full year in nine scenarios: three completion rates of planned renewable capacity by three electricity-demand levels.

When gas-fired backup power became much more expensive, the model built fuel cells and turned stored green hydrogen back into electricity.

Industry then drew more hydrogen from gas reformers, and the round trip from power to hydrogen and back lost energy, so total emissions rose although gas-plant emissions fell.

From: Li et al. (2025), International Journal of Hydrogen Energy.

Inputs reflect 2022–2023 conditions; post-2023 federal hydrogen credit rules are not modeled.
