Numbers and symbols

.gitignore

A file listing names and folders, such as keys and large raw data, that git add skips while they are untracked; it does not remove files already committed.

Learn in K8

95% confidence interval

A range worked out from the data by a method that, over many repeated samples, would contain the true value 95 times in 100.

Learn in M14

A

Absolute reference

A spreadsheet address with $ signs, such as $E$1, that stays the same when a formula is copied to other cells.

Learn in K7

Absorption chiller

A chiller driven by heat, such as steam or hot water, instead of an electric compressor; it uses a salt solution that absorbs and releases a refrigerant.

Learn in CF5

AC power flow (AC check)

A calculation of a network's voltages, currents and reactive power as well as its MW, from the alternating-current equations; it shows what a DC power flow or a first estimate leaves out.

Learn in A17

Additionality

Whether a purchase brings new clean generation that would not have been built otherwise, rather than claiming output that existed anyway.

Learn in A16

AFUE (annual fuel utilization efficiency)

The share of a furnace's fuel energy that leaves the furnace as heat over a heating season, under a standard test that leaves out duct losses and the fan's electricity.

Learn in F3

Agent-based model

A simulation in which many decision-makers, such as farmers or households, each follow their own rules, so that the system's behavior comes out of their choices.

Learn in CK-R

Aggregator

A company that enrolls many customers' devices, offers their combined response to a grid operator or utility, and shares the payment with the owners.

Learn in A7

Alkaline electrolyzer

An electrolyzer that uses an alkaline liquid electrolyte to carry ions between its electrodes, with operating limits that depend on the equipment design.

Learn in A16

Ancillary services

Services besides energy that keep the grid steady, such as reserves and frequency regulation; grid operators buy them separately from energy.

Learn in CF5

Annualized capital cost

A building cost spread into equal yearly amounts over the equipment's life, with interest, so it can be set against a year's revenue.

Learn in CF4

Annuity factor

What one dollar paid every year for a number of years is worth today, once each dollar is discounted.

Learn in K3

Anticipation

When people change their behavior before a program starts because they know it is coming.

Learn in M14

API key

A private code that identifies you to an online data service; treat it like a password and keep it out of shared files.

Learn in K8

Apparent power

The combined size of real and reactive power, the square root of MW² plus Mvar², in megavolt-amperes (MVA); a wire's heating follows it, not MW alone.

Learn in A17

Area median income (AMI)

The middle family income of an area, used to sort households into income bands such as 0-30% or 50-80% of it.

Learn in A13

Array

An ordered list or table of numbers; a linear program's costs c, rule table A and limits b are arrays.

Learn in B1

Assert

A line of code that stops a program with a message when a stated condition is false.

Learn in B2

Attributed emissions

The share of a grid's CO₂ assigned to one user, for example each MWh it uses times that hour's average emission factor.

Learn in CF6

Attributional analysis

An analysis that gives a product its share of today's burdens from average data, so the shares of all products add up.

Learn in A11

Audit trail

A record that lets someone follow a qualitative analysis from its raw material, such as interview notes, to each finding.

Learn in R5

Automatic generation control (AGC)

The grid operator's computer system that raises or lowers plants over minutes to bring frequency back to 60 Hz.

Learn in lesson 3

Autoregressive multi-fidelity model

A linear way to combine two sources: the accurate curve is modeled as a scaled copy of the rough curve plus a learned correction.

Learn in CF8

Average emission factor

An hour's CO₂ from all the plants that ran, divided by all the electricity used in that hour.

Learn in CF6

Average power

The energy used over a period divided by the length of that period.

Learn in lesson 1

B

Balancing

Keeping the power that plants make equal to the power that people use, at every moment.

Learn in lesson 0

Balancing authority

The organization that keeps supply and demand balanced, moment to moment, in its own part of the grid.

Learn in lesson 0

Balancing loop

A feedback loop with an odd number of negative links, which tends to oppose the change that started it; with long delays it can overshoot or swing back and forth.

Learn in F2

Baseline

An estimate of what a customer would have used without a demand-response event, against which the cut is measured.

Learn in A7

Baseline forecast

A simple rule, such as same hour yesterday, that any forecasting model must beat on the same hours to be worth using.

Learn in M10

Baseload operation

Operating a plant at a relatively steady output to supply the portion of demand that persists through the day.

Learn in CF4

Basis risk

The risk that the price where a plant sells differs from the price at which its contract settles, such as its node against a trading hub.

Learn in A2

Battery duration

How many hours a battery can discharge at its rated power: its energy in MWh divided by its power in MW.

Learn in N1

Battery margin

What a battery earns per megawatt-hour it discharges, after paying for the power it charged and the energy lost on the way.

Learn in A2

Benchmark

A case whose right answer is known in advance, from a hand calculation or an independent source, used to test a model.

Learn in R5

Benefit per ton

An estimate of the health harm, in dollars, avoided by not emitting one more ton of a pollutant from a given kind of source, averaged over where those sources are.

Learn in F11

Bias

The average signed miss of a forecast, showing whether it tends to run too high or too low.

Learn in lesson 4

Bidding zone (price zone)

An area of a power market in which every seller and buyer gets the same wholesale price for each trading period, as in Europe's markets.

Learn in F10

Billion cubic feet (Bcf)

A unit of natural-gas volume, a billion cubic feet; EIA often gives gas flows in Bcf a day.

Learn in X2

Binding constraint

A rule that holds exactly at the selected plan, with no room to spare; loosening it may or may not improve the best cost.

Learn in lesson 10

Boolean operators

AND, OR and NOT, the words that join search terms: AND narrows a search, OR widens it, NOT leaves terms out.

Learn in R2

Boundary condition (of a finding)

The place, time, data and assumptions a result holds under, stated next to it so readers do not stretch it further.

Learn in R8

Branch

A movable label on a line of commits, so a what-if can grow without changing the main line of work.

Learn in K8

Branch and bound

A search for the best whole-number plan that solves relaxations, splits on a fractional decision (off or on), and drops every branch whose bound cannot beat the best plan found.

Learn in M2

Break-even price

The sale price at which a project's net present value is exactly zero; when electricity is its only revenue, with the same costs, output and discounting, it equals the plant's levelized cost.

Learn in A1

Bridge power

Temporary on-site generation that serves part of a load until its grid connection is ready.

Learn in CF7

Btu (British thermal unit)

A unit of heat, the energy that warms one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit; one kilowatt-hour equals 3,412 Btu.

Learn in F5

Buffer (GIS)

The zone within a set distance of a map feature, such as everything within 1 km of a wind turbine.

Learn in R4

Building code

The rules a city, county or state sets for how new buildings must be built, such as insulation, wiring and safety.

Learn in F1

Building envelope

A building's outer shell, its walls, roof, windows and floor, whose insulation and air sealing set how fast heat leaks out.

Learn in A15

Bulk power system

The large power plants and the high-voltage transmission network that carry electricity over long distances, as distinct from the local distribution grid.

Learn in X1

Bus

A point of the network where plants, loads and lines meet, such as a substation; a network model has one balance rule per bus.

Learn in M1

C

CAIDI

Customer Average Interruption Duration Index: the average minutes to restore one interruption, SAIDI divided by SAIFI.

Learn in F6

Calibration (of a band)

A band is calibrated when it holds what it claims: a 90% band holds about 90% of new outcomes, overall and within groups that matter.

Learn in M9

Capacity

The most power a plant can produce at once, often called its nameplate size.

Learn in lesson 5

Capacity accreditation

The share of a resource's MW that a grid counts toward its adequacy target, judged by how much the resource lowers the risk of a shortfall.

Learn in A5

Capacity bound

The largest size a model is allowed to choose for a piece of equipment.

Learn in CF4

Capacity credit

The share of a plant's size that planners count on to meet the year's peak, judged by how much it lowers the risk of a shortage.

Learn in CK-M

Capacity expansion model

An optimization model that decides what to build, as well as how to run it, at least cost.

Learn in lesson 13

Capacity factor

The energy a plant actually made over a period, divided by the energy it would have made running at full capacity the whole time.

Learn in lesson 5

Capacity market

An auction, run ahead of time, that pays plants and other resources for being available, in an amount a planner sets, on top of energy prices.

Learn in A3

Capacity payment

Money a plant earns for being available to run when needed, as in a capacity market, apart from the energy it sells.

Learn in CF4

Capacity utilization

The energy a machine used or made over a year divided by its size times 8,760 hours, its average loading, so half power all year gives 50%, as full power for half the year does.

Learn in M11

Capital cost

The money spent to build a power plant, usually given per kilowatt of capacity, before it makes any electricity.

Learn in lesson 7

Capital recovery factor (CRF)

The share of a building cost that must be repaid each year, with interest, to pay it off over a given number of years.

Learn in lesson 7

Capital stack

The layers of money that pay for a project, such as senior debt, tax equity and the sponsor's equity, each with its own place in the order of payment.

Learn in X5

Capture price

The average market price a plant's output earns, each hour weighted by what the plant produced in it.

Learn in A3

Capture ratio

A market price averaged over the hours a plant produces, weighted by its output, divided by that price's average over all hours.

Learn in A2

Carbon budget

A cap on the total CO₂ emitted over many years, a limit on a stock rather than on one year's flow.

Learn in F2

Carbon capture and storage (CCS)

Capturing CO₂ from a source such as a chimney before it reaches the air, and storing it, usually deep underground.

Learn in X2

Carbon dioxide removal (CDR)

Taking CO₂ that is already in the air and storing it in trees, soils, rock, minerals or products.

Learn in X2

Carbon intensity

The CO2 emitted for each unit of electricity, for example in tonnes per megawatt-hour.

Learn in lesson 12

Carbon price

A charge per tonne of CO2 emitted, added to costs so that cleaner choices become relatively cheaper.

Learn in lesson 12

Carnot limit

The largest share of heat any heat engine can turn into work: 1 minus the cold temperature over the hot temperature, both in kelvin.

Learn in F4

Carrying cost

What money tied up in buildings or equipment costs while they earn nothing: the interest, or the return it could have earned elsewhere.

Learn in CF7

Cash flow available for debt service (CFADS)

A project's revenue minus its operating costs in a period: the cash that can pay the loan.

Learn in X5

Causal-loop diagram

A map of causes and effects in which every arrow carries a sign: plus if more of the cause brings more of the effect, minus if it brings less.

Learn in F2

Chain reaction

A run of fissions in which the neutrons from each split go on to split more atoms; a reactor holds it steady, with each generation as large as the last.

Learn in F5

Chance constraint

A rule required to hold with a chosen probability, for example at least a 95% chance of meeting a week's demand, rather than with certainty.

Learn in CF1

Checksum

A short fingerprint computed from a file's bytes, such as SHA-256, that changes if even one byte of the file changes.

Learn in K8

Choice experiment

A survey method in which people choose repeatedly between offers whose features vary, so that the weight of each feature can be estimated.

Learn in X6

Circuit breaker

A switch that opens by itself when it senses a fault, cutting the power to everything beyond it, and can be closed again.

Learn in F6

Clean clone

A fresh clone of a repository into a new, empty folder, run with only what the repository holds, so that no file left on your own machine can change the result.

Learn in K8

Clean-energy standard

A rule that a set share of the electricity sold in a year comes from sources the rule counts as clean; sellers prove it with certificates, each a record of one MWh from a counted source.

Learn in A12

Clearing price

The price at which supply meets demand in a market; in a uniform-price market, every seller that runs in that hour is paid it.

Learn in lesson 8

Clone

A complete new copy of a repository, with its files and its whole history.

Learn in K8

Cluster study

A study of every interconnection request in one window together, which finds the network upgrades the group needs and splits their cost.

Learn in A6

Clustering

Grouping similar items, such as days with similar demand and weather, so that one representative can stand for each group.

Learn in R2

Co-op (electric cooperative)

An electric utility owned by the members it serves, common in rural areas.

Learn in R1

Co-optimize

To choose several decisions together in one model, so each is set with the others in view rather than one after another.

Learn in CF5

Coefficient of performance (COP)

Heat a heat pump delivers divided by the electricity it uses; a COP of 3 means 3 kWh of heat for each kWh of electricity.

Learn in F3

Cogeneration

Making two useful products from one fuel or heat source, such as electricity and heat, steam or cooling, instead of making each separately.

Learn in CF5

Coincident peak

The most a group of customers draws in the same hour; it is lower than the sum of their own peaks when they peak at different times.

Learn in A15

Combined-cycle plant

A gas power plant that burns gas in a turbine and uses the hot exhaust to make steam for a second turbine, so it gets more electricity from the same fuel.

Learn in lesson 0

Commit

A recorded snapshot of a project's tracked files as they were staged, with a message, its author and time, and a link to the commit before it.

Learn in K8

Compound hazard

Two or more hazards striking at the same time, such as heat and drought, whose combined harm can exceed the sum of each alone.

Learn in A9

Concentrated solar power

Mirrors focus sunlight to heat a fluid; the heat runs a turbine or a process, and it can be stored for hours in tanks.

Learn in CF2

Concept matrix

A table with papers in rows and ideas in columns, coded by a stated rule, that shows what a literature covers and what it leaves out.

Learn in R2

Condenser

The part of a steam plant that cools the used steam back into water, so it can be pumped back to the boiler; most of the plant's waste heat leaves here.

Learn in F4

Condensing furnace

A furnace that recovers additional heat by condensing water vapor in its exhaust, with efficiency depending on its design and operating conditions.

Learn in A18

Conditional price

A price read from a linear program in which the on/off decisions are held fixed; it holds only for that commitment.

Learn in M2

Conflict of interest

A financial or other tie that could bias, or seem to bias, a researcher's work, and so must be declared.

Learn in R9

Confounding

When something other than the program, such as a second change at the same time, also differs between the groups and moves the outcome.

Learn in M14

Congestion

A power line carrying all it is allowed to, so cheaper power cannot reach where it is needed and prices can differ by location.

Learn in lesson 8

Congestion rent

What loads pay at their locational prices minus what generators are paid at theirs; in a DC model it equals the sum, over the full lines, of each line's shadow price times its flow.

Learn in M1

Consequential analysis

An analysis of what changes because of a decision, in the short run through how plants run and in the long run through what gets built.

Learn in A10

Consequential emissions

The change in what power plants actually emit because of a decision, as the grid re-dispatches; it is counted with marginal, not average, emission factors.

Learn in A7

Constraint

A rule every solution must obey, such as supply equals demand or no plant above its size.

Learn in lesson 10

Control rod

A rod of neutron-absorbing material that is pushed into a reactor core to slow the chain reaction, or pulled out to speed it up.

Learn in F5

Control variable

A measured trait, such as home size, put into a regression so that groups are compared at equal values of it; it cannot fix a difference nobody measured.

Learn in M14

Coolant

The fluid, such as water, helium, liquid sodium or molten salt, that carries heat away from a reactor core.

Learn in F5

Coordinate reference system (CRS)

The rule that ties map coordinates to places on Earth; some keep areas true, others keep distances, none keeps both everywhere.

Learn in R4

Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)

The world reference clock, with no daylight saving; EIA stamps its hourly grid data in it.

Learn in B2

Coordination cost

What it costs to enroll, meter, control and pay each device or customer in a program, whatever the device's size.

Learn in A7

Correlated failure

Many parts of a system failing together because they share one cause, such as extreme cold freezing plants and gas supply at once.

Learn in A9

Cost of capital

The yearly return an owner's lenders and investors require on money tied up in equipment; the real cost of capital leaves inflation out.

Learn in M5

Cost of saved energy

What a measure costs per unit of energy it saves: its extra cost spread over its life at a discount rate, plus any extra yearly cost, divided by the energy it saves each year.

Learn in A15

Cost of service

What it costs a utility to serve a customer or a class of customers: energy, plants, wires, meters and billing.

Learn in A4

Cost per tonne avoided

A measure's net yearly cost, its capital spread over its life plus upkeep minus the energy it saves, divided by the tonnes of CO2 it avoids a year.

Learn in A18

Cost surface

A map that gives the cost of building a line or a road through each cell, for example in dollars per km.

Learn in A19

Counterfactual

What would have happened without the change being judged; every saving or effect is measured against one.

Learn in N1

Coverage

The share of actual outcomes that land inside a forecast's band; on its own it rewards wide bands.

Learn in M10

CO₂e (carbon dioxide equivalent)

Greenhouse gases such as methane counted as the mass of CO₂ that would warm the climate as much over a set period, so they can be added up.

Learn in A16

CRediT contributor roles

A standard list of 14 names for what each author did, such as conceptualization (shaping the question), data curation (cleaning and documenting data) and formal analysis.

Learn in R9

Critical (of a reactor)

The steady state of a chain reaction, with a multiplication factor of exactly 1; it is a reactor's normal operating state, not an emergency.

Learn in F5

Critical load

The loads that must stay on in an outage, such as a hospital or a water plant; the rest can be switched off.

Learn in F7

Cross-subsidy

When some customers pay more than the cost of serving them so that others can pay less.

Learn in A4

CRPS

The continuous ranked probability score of a whole forecast distribution: twice its pinball loss averaged over every quantile level; lower is better.

Learn in M9

Crystallization (in an absorption chiller)

The salt solution in an absorption chiller turning to solid crystals when it gets too concentrated or too cold, which blocks the machine; the chiller must stay clear of that limit.

Learn in CF5

Cube law

The power carried by the wind grows with the cube of its speed (twice the speed, eight times the power); a real turbine's output follows it only roughly, and only between its cut-in and rated speeds.

Learn in lesson 5

Curtailment (spilling)

Turning a wind or solar farm below what the weather allows, usually because the grid has more power than it can use at that moment.

Learn in lesson 0

Customer class

A group of customers, such as homes or businesses, that a utility bills under the same tariffs.

Learn in A4

Customer-hours

Customers without power times the hours they are out: 100 customers out for 3 hours is 300 customer-hours.

Learn in R10

Customer-minutes

The number of customers without power times the minutes they wait: 10 homes dark for an hour is 600 customer-minutes.

Learn in F6

Cut-in speed

The lowest wind speed at which a turbine starts to make electricity.

Learn in lesson 5

Cut-out speed

The wind speed above which a turbine shuts down to protect itself.

Learn in lesson 5

Cut-out temperature (heat pump)

The outdoor temperature below which a heat pump's compressor, the pump that moves its heat, switches off, so a backup heater must take over.

Learn in F3

D

Data center

A building full of computers that store and process data; on the grid it is one large customer that draws power around the clock.

Learn in F8

Data leakage

Letting a model use information it would not have when the forecast is made, such as a later hour's actual value; it makes scores look better than they can be.

Learn in M10

Data license

The terms under which a dataset may be used and shared, such as public domain, CC BY or a signed data agreement.

Learn in R4

Data note

A short record of where a dataset came from, its license, units and time base, and every cleaning step with its rule, count and effect.

Learn in R4

DataFrame

A pandas table with named columns and an index, such as one row per hour.

Learn in B2

Day-ahead forecast

A grid operator's estimate, made the day before, of how much power customers will use in each hour.

Learn in lesson 4

Daylight saving time

Clocks moved an hour ahead in summer, giving one 23-hour day in spring and one 25-hour day in autumn.

Learn in B2

DC power flow

A linear approximation of real-power flows that sets each line's flow from the voltage-angle difference across it, holds voltage magnitudes fixed, assumes small angles, and leaves out losses and reactive power.

Learn in M1

Debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR)

The cash available for debt service divided by the loan payment due; a lender asks for a minimum, such as 1.30, as a cushion.

Learn in X5

Decay heat

Heat that the radioactive fission products in used fuel keep giving off after the chain reaction has stopped.

Learn in F5

Decision analysis

A structured way to choose among options under uncertainty, weighing each possible outcome by how likely and how valuable it is.

Learn in R10

Decision variable

A quantity the model is free to choose, such as how much each plant makes each hour.

Learn in lesson 10

Deep uncertainty

A situation where the people involved do not know, or cannot agree on, the possible outcomes, their odds or the right model.

Learn in R6

Delay cost per MW-month

A delay's cost in present value divided by the load's size in MW and by the months it waits, so waits of different lengths and loads of different sizes can be compared.

Learn in CF7

Deliverability

Whether power from a plant can actually reach the buyer over the grid, usually judged by both being in the same region.

Learn in A16

Demand (load)

The total power that all users on a grid draw at a given moment.

Learn in lesson 0

Demand charge

A part of a power bill charged per kW of the customer's busiest hour (or interval) in the month, not per kWh.

Learn in A4

Demand response

Electricity users cutting or shifting their use when the grid asks, usually in return for a payment.

Learn in lesson 6

Derate

A cut in the most a power plant can produce while it keeps running, for example when its cooling water is too warm or its equipment freezes.

Learn in A9

Desalination

Removing salt from seawater or salty groundwater to make fresh water, with heat or with electricity.

Learn in F5

Design day

A cold (or hot) day chosen for planning, so that equipment and networks are sized for its highest hour rather than for an average day.

Learn in A15

Deterministic equivalent

A chance constraint rewritten as an ordinary rule with a safety margin, for example planning for the demand level that is exceeded with only the allowed probability.

Learn in CF1

Developer

The company that plans, builds and owns a new facility, such as a factory or a data center.

Learn in CF7

Diff

A line-by-line list of what changed between two versions of a file, with removed lines marked minus and added lines plus.

Learn in K8

Difference-in-differences

A comparison of how much an outcome changed in a group that got a program against how much it changed in a similar group that did not.

Learn in R3

Diffusion curve

How the share of homes, firms or output using a new technology grows over time, often slowly at first, then faster, then leveling off.

Learn in X3

Discount rate

The yearly rate used to turn money paid or received in the future into its value today.

Learn in lesson 7

Discounted cash flow

Valuing a project's future yearly cash flows in today's money, each discounted at a chosen rate; their sum is the project's net present value.

Learn in CF4

Dispatch

Deciding how much power each plant should make, hour by hour or minute by minute.

Learn in lesson 0

Dispatchable plant

A power plant whose output the operator can turn up or down on request, such as a gas plant.

Learn in lesson 0

Distribution

The lower-voltage part of the grid, from substations along feeders and laterals to homes and businesses.

Learn in F6

Distribution factor

The share of a project's output that would flow on a given line, used to split the cost of upgrading that line.

Learn in A6

District heating

Heat made in one place and piped as hot water or steam to warm many buildings.

Learn in F5

Dominated plan

A plan that another plan beats on every count: no more cost, no more emissions, and better on at least one.

Learn in lesson 12

Dot product

The sum of the products of two vectors' matching entries, such as costs times outputs, added up.

Learn in K6

Duck curve

The daily shape of demand minus solar output, with a dip at midday and a steep climb in the evening.

Learn in lesson 2

Dummy variable

A 0/1 flag in a regression, such as 1 for a home on the tariff and 0 for one that is not; its coefficient is the fitted difference between the two groups.

Learn in M14

Duration curve

A year of hourly values sorted from highest to lowest, showing for how many hours each level is reached.

Learn in B2

E

Econometrics

Statistics applied to economic data, often to estimate whether a change caused an effect.

Learn in R10

Efficiency

The share of the energy put into a plant or device that comes out in the useful form, such as electricity.

Learn in K2

EIA

The Energy Information Administration, the statistics agency of the United States Department of Energy, which publishes data on energy production, use and prices.

Learn in CF3

Elective pay

A rule of United States tax law that lets tax-exempt and governmental bodies, such as school districts, receive the value of certain clean-energy tax credits as a payment from the IRS.

Learn in CK-A

Electric current

The flow of electric charge through a wire, measured in amperes (A); for the same power, a higher voltage needs less current.

Learn in F6

Electric vehicle (EV)

A car, bus or truck driven by an electric motor from a battery charged from the grid, instead of by burning gasoline or diesel.

Learn in SB3

Electrification

Switching a use of energy, such as heating or driving, from burning fuel to using electricity.

Learn in lesson 2

Electrolyzer

A machine that uses electricity to split water into hydrogen and oxygen.

Learn in CF2

Electrolyzer stack

The stack of cells inside an electrolyzer where electricity splits water into hydrogen and oxygen; it wears with use and is replaced several times over a plant's life.

Learn in M11

Emission factor

The average mass of a pollutant released per unit of fuel burned or of output, such as kilograms of SO₂ per tonne of coal, taken from measurements.

Learn in F11

Emissions cap

A limit on the total CO2 that may be emitted in a period, such as a year.

Learn in lesson 12

Emissions inventory

An account that divides emissions that already happened among their sources or users, so that the shares add up to the total.

Learn in A10

Employment multiplier

The total jobs, on site plus at suppliers and from workers' spending, that go with each on-site job, taken from a regional input-output table.

Learn in A13

Energization

Switching on a customer's grid service, so that power can flow to it.

Learn in CF7

Energy

An amount of electricity made or used over a stretch of time, equal to power multiplied by time and measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh) or megawatt-hours (MWh).

Learn in lesson 0

Energy attribute certificate (EAC)

A tradable record that one megawatt-hour was generated by a given plant at a given place and time, which a buyer retires to claim that power; a rule decides which certificates may count.

Learn in M11

Energy burden

The share of a household's income spent on energy bills.

Learn in A22

Energy carrier

A form in which energy is moved to where it is used, such as electricity, natural gas, gasoline or hydrogen.

Learn in F3

Energy hub

A model of energy carriers coming in, being converted and stored, and meeting the uses inside a chosen boundary: a building, a campus, a district or a city.

Learn in SB3

Energy security

Keeping energy flowing at bearable prices when a fuel, a supplier or a route is cut off.

Learn in A22

Energy service

What people actually want from energy, such as a warm room, hot water, cold food, light or a trip; the energy is only the means.

Learn in F3

Energy storage

Equipment, such as a battery, that takes in electricity at one time and gives it back later, with some losses.

Learn in lesson 9

Energy-only market

A wholesale market design with no separate capacity market, in which energy and reserve prices, high in scarce hours, must pay for capacity that is rarely used.

Synthetic data

Invented values made to look realistic, used in place of real records so that a model can be shown and shared.

Learn in CF1

Synthetic sample

A few invented records with the same columns and realistic values as data that cannot be shared, so others can run the code without the real data.

Learn in R9

System boundary

The line that decides which parts, costs and emissions a study counts and which it leaves out.

Learn in N1

T

Target-seeking scenario

A scenario that starts from a goal, such as a carbon budget, and works out what it would take to meet it; the IEA calls this kind normative.

Learn in A20

Tariff

The rule for calculating electricity bills: fixed charges, prices per kWh, or both.

Learn in A22

Tax base

The total value a local government can tax, such as the assessed value of homes, businesses and power plants in a county.

Learn in A13

Tax equity

Money from an investor with a large tax bill, put into a project in exchange for most of its tax credits and depreciation, plus some cash.

Learn in X5

Techno-economic analysis (TEA)

Estimating what a technology costs and earns from its engineering design, its prices and its operating pattern.

Learn in R10

Techno-economic optimization

A model that compares equipment sizes and ways of running them by both their performance and their cost, and picks the best for a stated goal.

Learn in F7

Technology readiness level (TRL)

A scale from 1 (basic principles described) to 9 (proven in operation) for how mature a technology or method is.

Learn in X2

Telemetry

Measurements sent automatically from plants and substations to the grid operator.

Learn in B2

Temperature lift

How far a heat pump raises heat: its delivery temperature minus its source temperature, in kelvin (a difference of 1 K equals 1 °C).

Learn in A15

Terawatt-hour (TWh)

A unit of energy equal to a billion kilowatt-hours, used for the yearly electricity use of regions and countries.

Learn in lesson 1

Thermal rating

The most current, or MVA at the line's voltage, that a line or transformer can carry before its wires get too hot and sag.

Learn in A17

Thermal storage

Storing energy as heat, for example in tanks of hot molten salt, to use it hours later.

Learn in CF2

Tie rule

A stated way to pick one answer when several plans cost exactly the same, usually tiny costs that are left out of the reported results.

Learn in SB3

Time slice

A group of hours, such as winter nights, that a planning model treats as one, weighted by how many hours it stands for.

Learn in SB4

Time step

The length of one step in a model or dataset, such as five minutes, an hour or a year.

Learn in R3

Time-of-use tariff

A retail electricity price that changes with the hour of the day, higher at peak times and lower off-peak.

Learn in A4

Time-series aggregation

Shrinking a long series, such as a year of hours, to a few representative periods that a model can solve quickly.

Learn in R2

Tolling agreement

A contract in which the buyer pays the owner of a plant a fixed fee for the right to run it, and keeps what it earns.

Learn in A2

Ton of coal equivalent (tce)

An energy unit equal to the heat in one metric ton of standard coal, fixed by convention at 29.3076 GJ; China's energy statistics count in it.

Learn in F10

Ton of oil equivalent (toe)

An energy unit equal to the heat in one metric ton of crude oil, fixed by convention at 41.868 GJ; ktoe is a thousand of them, Mtoe (Brazil's Mtep) a million.

Learn in F10

Tornado chart

A chart that ranks inputs by how far a result moves when each input is swung from a low to a high value, one at a time.

Learn in lesson 7

Tracked file

A file Git already knows, because it is in the last commit or the staging area; a new file stays untracked until git add.

Learn in K8

Trading hub

A pricing point where many contracts are settled, priced as an average over many nodes of the grid.

Learn in A2

Transactive energy

Buying and selling electricity among many small participants, such as homes and firms with their own generation, as well as with the grid.

Learn in A7

Transfer

A payment that moves money from one group to another, such as a carbon payment to the government or a credit to a plant owner, without using anything up.

Learn in A12

Transformer

A device with two coils that raises or lowers an alternating voltage, such as from 18 kV to 345 kV at a plant or from 12.47 kV to 240/120 V near a home.

Learn in F6

Transmission

The high-voltage part of the grid (115 to 765 kV in the U.S.) that carries power over long distances between plants, regions and substations.

Learn in F6

Transport model

A network model in which each link can carry any flow up to its limit, with no power-flow physics: power goes where the cheapest routes allow.

Learn in SB2

Triangulation

Checking a finding against a second source or a second method, so that it does not rest on one kind of evidence alone.

Learn in CR

Truncated axis

A chart axis that starts above zero; on a bar chart it makes differences look larger than they are.

Learn in R8

Turbine

A machine whose blades are pushed round by moving gas, steam, water or wind, turning a shaft.

Learn in F4

Turbine crossover

The pipe that carries steam from a high-pressure turbine to a low-pressure turbine; steam taken there has already done part of its work.

Learn in CF5

Two-stage stochastic program

An optimization model that chooses the here-and-now decisions and each branch's recourse together, to minimize the expected cost over all branches.

Learn in M8

U

Unbounded

Said of a model whose goal can improve without limit, which usually means a rule is missing.

Learn in lesson 10

Unbundling

Separating the grid from the making and selling of electricity, so that different companies, or legally separate parts of one, run each.

Learn in F10

Under-frequency load shedding

Relays that automatically disconnect blocks of customers when the frequency falls to a set level, to save the rest of the grid.

Learn in lesson 3

Unit commitment

Deciding which plants are on in each hour, before deciding how much each one produces.

Learn in lesson 11

Unserved energy

Demand that could not be met, so some customers lost power.

Learn in lesson 0

Uplift payment

A payment outside the market price that covers costs a scheduled plant cannot recover at the price, such as start-up and no-load costs; also called a make-whole payment.

Learn in M2

Uprate (of a line or link)

Raising the most power a line or link may carry, for example with new conductors or a second circuit.

Learn in SB2

Uranium-235

The form of uranium whose atoms split easily when they catch a slow neutron; natural uranium holds only a small share of it.

Learn in F5

Useful energy

Energy a device delivers in the wanted form, such as heat, light or motion; a heat pump's delivered heat includes heat drawn from the outdoor air, not only the energy bought.

Learn in F3

Utility-scale

Large enough to sell power to the grid; in EIA's statistics, plants of at least 1 MW.

Learn in R8

UTM (Universal Transverse Mercator)

A set of 60 north-to-south zones, each a flat map in meters that keeps distances close to true inside the zone.

Learn in R4

V

Validation

Checking that a model represents the real system well enough for the question it is used to answer.

Learn in B3

Value of coupling

How much cheaper a system is with devices that link its carriers than with each carrier supplied on its own, judged as a difference of optimal costs.

Learn in M4

Value of information

How much a decision-maker would gain, on average, by learning an uncertain quantity before deciding; its upper limit is the value of a perfect forecast.

Learn in R6

Value of lost load

What a customer loses when power is cut, per hour or per kWh not delivered; it differs by customer and by how long the outage lasts.

Learn in A14

Value of the stochastic solution (VSS)

How much the stochastic program's plan saves, in expectation, against the plan built for the average future.

Learn in M8

Variable O&M

Operation and maintenance costs, apart from fuel, that grow with every megawatt-hour a plant makes.

Learn in lesson 7

Variable renewables

Wind and solar power, whose output follows the weather: they can be turned down but not up.

Learn in lesson 0

Variogram

A measure of how much values at two places differ, on average, as a function of the distance between them; here, across a wind plant's turbines.

Learn in CF8

Vector

An ordered list of numbers, such as a plan's outputs, in which the position of each number carries its meaning.

Learn in K6

Verification

Checking that a model's equations and code implement the intended model correctly, with tests such as units, the energy balance and hand-solved cases.

Learn in B3

Version control

Keeping selected recorded versions of a project's files, with who changed what and why, so any recorded version can be compared or brought back.

Learn in K8

Version pin

A line such as pandas==2.2.3 in requirements.txt that asks the installer for one exact version of a package.

Learn in K8

Vertically integrated utility

An electric utility that owns and runs the power plants, transmission and distribution in its service territory, as Kansas's investor-owned utilities do; its rates are approved by the state regulator.

Learn in F9

Vintage

The year a plant was built: in a multi-decade model each vintage keeps its own cost and runs until its life ends.

Learn in SB4

Virtual power plant

Many small devices, such as batteries, thermostats and chargers, controlled together and offered to the grid as one resource.

Learn in A7

Voltage

The electrical push that drives current through a wire, measured in volts (V); 1 kilovolt (kV) is 1,000 V.

Learn in F6

Voltage angle

How far the alternating voltage at one bus runs ahead of the voltage at the reference bus, whose angle is set to zero; in DC power flow a line's flow is proportional to the angle difference across it.

Learn in M1

W

Wait-and-see problem

The planning problem solved as if the future were known: one best plan for each branch, which no real planner can choose in advance.

Learn in M8

Waste heat

Heat a power plant cannot turn into electricity and releases to the air or to cooling water.

Learn in F4

Water consumption

Water taken and not returned, mostly lost to evaporation, so it is no longer available downstream.

Learn in A11

Water withdrawal

Water taken from a river, lake or aquifer, of which the part not returned is consumption; how much returns depends on the cooling system.

Learn in A11

Watt-hour (Wh)

A unit of energy: one watt kept up for one hour; 1,000 Wh make one kilowatt-hour.

Learn in lesson 1

Weather zone

One of the areas into which a grid operator divides its region for weather and demand forecasts; ERCOT has eight.

Learn in CF3

Weatherization

Air sealing, insulation and similar work on a building's shell that cuts the heat it loses, so it needs less heating.

Learn in A18

Web Mercator

The projection most web maps use: good for viewing, but it stretches distances and areas more the farther a place is from the equator.

Learn in R4

Weibull distribution

A curve often used for the share of a year's hours at each wind speed; its shape k (no unit) sets how spread out the speeds are, and its scale c, in m/s, sets how high they run.

Learn in CF8

Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)

The blended return a project must pay its lenders and its owners, used as the project's discount rate.

Learn in lesson 7

Weighted overlay

Combining several criterion maps cell by cell: each criterion is scored from 0 to 1, multiplied by its weight, and the results are added into one suitability map.

Learn in A19

Weighted sum

A way to combine several goals into one score by adding them up, each multiplied by a weight.

Learn in lesson 12

Well-to-gate

An emissions boundary from producing the inputs, such as gas or electricity, to hydrogen leaving the plant; delivery and use are outside it.

Learn in A16

Wet-bulb temperature

The lowest temperature evaporating water can reach in the air at hand, which combines heat and humidity; when it is high, chillers and cooling towers reject heat less well and deliver less, which is called derating.

Learn in CF5

Wholesale price

The price at which electricity is traded between power plants and the companies that sell it to customers, often set hour by hour.

Learn in lesson 8

Wildfire safety shutoff

A utility switching off power lines in dry, windy weather so that they cannot start a fire; the customers on those lines lose power.

Learn in A9

Willans line

A straight-line rule for a turbine's steam use against its output; here it gives the electricity lost for each unit of steam taken out of the turbine.

Learn in CF5

Winterization

Protecting power plants and fuel supply against freezing, with insulation, electric heaters on exposed pipes (heat tracing), enclosures and cold-weather procedures.
