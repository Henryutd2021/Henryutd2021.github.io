Techno-economic analysis inside an optimization model

Put a wind-hydrogen plant’s capital, a tax credit in its own years and the credit’s emissions tiers into one LP, then move the boundary and watch the conclusion change

Modeler lesson M11 of Energy Systems, Step by Step: link an hourly linear program of a wind-hydrogen plant to its lifetime cash flows, annualize capital with the capital recovery factor, add the U.S. hydrogen production credit as current law states it, in its own ten years and with its emissions tiers, then move the plant’s boundary and see the size, the NPV and the cost per kilogram change; a Python notebook rebuilds the model.

With JavaScript on, you solve each step's linear program in the browser.

Illustrative plant and prices. A new 100 MW wind farm behind a 50 MW connection, trading at Prairie Town's cost of one more MWh ($0, $40 or $100, lesson 9); a hydrogen buyer and a delivery charge you set. Real 2025 dollars.

Real shapes, 36 days. Wind and prices follow the Southwest Power Pool's 2025 hours (EIA-930), wind at EIA's 33.8% capacity factor. Three days per month stand for the year.

Checked on the whole year. The lesson's test re-runs the plans on all 8,760 hours of 2025: hydrogen within 4%, NPV within $4M.

Costs. Electrolyzer: DOE Record 24005 (May 2024), $2,000 per kW in 2022 dollars, $2,184 per kW in 2025 dollars by BEA's GDP deflator; upkeep 5% a year; stack wear $6.01 per MWh. Wind farm, rate and life: lesson 13.

About 40 guided minutes in nine steps. Solver: HiGHS (open source), loaded on your first solve (about 3.5 MB, once).

Build it in Python · notebook lab, about 45 minutes, then 90 minutes of practice

The same LP in scipy.optimize.linprog(method="highs"): the representative days, the capital recovery factor, the credit's two periods and the tiers. assert cells check your sizes, hydrogen, NPVs, costs per kg and credit per kg against the lesson's reference block.

One representative day's hours are printed, not asserted: other plans of the same value can run some hours differently.

What you just did, as diagrams

Capital in the goal
Each MW costs its capital recovery and upkeep every year: CRF 0.0578 at 4% real over 30 years.
Size and hours together
Without a credit the LP builds 22.7 MW: a higher cost per kg than 30 MW, yet more value.
A credit in its own years
$3.185 per kg for 10 of 30 years is worth about $1.24/kg over the life: 3.185 × 0.388, the credit years' 38.8% of the discounted kilograms.
Why 38.8%, not 47%
The credit years are 47% of the discounted years, but the plant makes 4.96 million kg a year under the tier's cap and 6.91 million after it, when it buys more grid power.
A step rule as a set of LPs
One LP per emissions tier, each with a cap on counted grid CO₂ per kg; the top tier wins here, at 46.2 MW.
New books, same plant
One plant or the electrolyzer's increment: the same 46.2 MW plan, worth $46.4M or $121.2M. The increment leaves out the wind farm on its own, worth −$74.8M.
A new layout, a new plan
Across the grid the electrolyzer buys every MWh and pays the charge: 40.1 MW, worth $63.2M against the same wind farm alone, where the increment is worth $121.2M.
Go deeper · ModelerThe plant's LP in algebra

Periods \(p\) (the credit’s years, then the rest), representative days \(d\) with weights \(n_d\) (365 in all), hours \(t = 0 \dots 23\); MW over an hour is MWh. Money is an annual equivalent in real 2025 dollars:

\[\min\; (\mathit{CRF} + f)\, c\, S \;+\; \sum_p \varphi_p \sum_{d,t} n_d \Big[ (p_t + q)\, \mathit{imp} - p_t\, \mathit{exp} + \big(v - (h + \kappa_p)\, k\big)\, \mathit{ez} \Big]\]

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathit{dw} + \mathit{exp} &\le w_t, \quad \mathit{ez} = \mathit{dw} + \mathit{imp} \le S, \quad \mathit{exp}, \mathit{imp} \le 50, \quad x_t = \mathit{exp} && \text{(each hour, behind the meter)}\\ \mathit{dw} = \mathit{exp} &= 0, \quad \mathit{ez} = \mathit{imp} \le S, \quad x_t = \min(w_t, 50) && \text{(each hour, across the grid)}\\ \textstyle\sum_{d,t} n_d \big(e\, u - L\, k\, \mathit{ez} / 1000\big) &\le 0, \quad u \ge \mathit{imp} - x_t, \quad u \ge 0 && \text{(a tier, period 1)}\\ \mathit{dw}, \mathit{exp}, \mathit{imp} &\ge 0, \quad 0 \le S \le 100 && \text{(bounds)}\\ \varphi_1 = \mathit{AF}(r, 10) / \mathit{AF}(r, N), &\quad \varphi_2 = 1 - \varphi_1, \quad \mathit{CRF} = 1 / \mathit{AF}(r, N) && \text{(weights)} \end{aligned} \]

Symbol Meaning Unit
\(S\) the electrolyzer’s size (a decision, 0 to 100) MW
\(c\) installed cost: $2,184 per kW = about $2,184,000 per MW $/MW
\(f\) fixed upkeep: 5% of \(c\) every year share a year
\(\mathit{dw}\), \(\mathit{exp}\), \(\mathit{imp}\) wind to the electrolyzer, wind sold, power bought MW
\(\mathit{ez}\) power into the electrolyzer, \(\mathit{dw} + \mathit{imp}\) MW
\(p_t\), \(q\) Prairie Town’s price, the delivery charge ($10) $/MWh
\(v\) stack wear, $6.01 per MWh $/MWh
\(h\), \(\kappa_p\) hydrogen price ($4), the credit in period \(p\) ($3.185, then 0) $/kg
\(k\) \(1000 / 57\), about 17.54 kg/MWh
\(e\), \(L\) the grid’s CO₂ rate (0.082), the tier’s limit (0.449 for the top tier) t/MWh, kg/kg
\(x_t\) certificates from the same hour: the wind sold behind the meter; across the grid, the wind farm’s own sales MW
\(u\) power bought not matched in the same hour, never below 0 MW
\(\mathit{AF}(r, N)\) annuity factor, \((1 - (1+r)^{-N}) / r\) years
  • Bounds matter. Every power variable is \(\ge 0\). Without \(u \ge 0\), a negative \(u\) in an hour that sells would cancel power bought in another hour: annual matching, not hourly.
  • Two connections. Behind the plant’s meter, power bought and sold share the wind farm’s 50 MW connection. Across the grid, the electrolyzer has its own connection at the buyer and buys all its power, up to \(S\).
  • Two periods, one size. Period 1 is the credit’s 10 years, period 2 the rest of the life; both run on the same 36 days and share \(S\).
  • Counting rules. No certificates: \(u = \mathit{imp}\). Annual matching: the cap counts the year’s \(\sum n_d (\mathit{imp} - \mathit{exp})\) in one row; it may go below zero there, because the right side never does. Across the grid, \(\mathit{exp}\) is the wind farm’s own sales.
  • Behind one meter, the plant never buys and sells in the same hour (the tie rule), so hourly matching counts every MWh bought, as no certificates do: the page’s LP leaves \(u\) out there.
  • Size. About 5,200 columns and 3,500 rows with a tier; HiGHS solves it in a fraction of a second.
Go deeper · ModelerWhy one LP per tier
  • A step is not linear. The credit jumps from $0.635 to $3.185 per kg as the rate crosses limits; no single LP can hold the jump.
  • Enumerate the steps. Each tier fixes the amount and adds one linear cap; five LPs (no credit, four tiers). The largest value wins; of the cases within $1 a year of it, the one listed first.
  • Caps from above only. A case’s plan can be cleaner than its tier and earn less than it could. Such a case never wins: the cleaner tier’s LP holds the same plan and pays more.
  • A bound without an LP. With its cap removed, a case’s hours are independent once the size is set: a few lines of arithmetic give its best value, a bound on the capped case.
  • Skip what cannot win. A case needs no LP when its bound is more than $2 a year below the best LP so far ($1 for the tie, $1 for rounding), or no better than an earlier-listed solved case, which wins the tie (here, 4 of 5 skipped).
  • The alternative. A mixed-integer program with one binary per tier picks the tier itself (M2). Its solve gives no LP shadow prices directly; fixing its binaries and solving the LP that is left gives conditional ones.
  • What it is not. The amounts are the law’s; the lesson never varies them.
Go deeper · AnalystThe credit as the IRS states it
  • Who. A facility whose construction begins before 1 January 2028; Public Law 119-21 (2025) ended the credit for later starts. Paid for the 10 years from the day it is placed in service.
  • What. Hydrogen with at most 4 kg CO₂e per kg, well-to-gate, from DOE’s 45VH2-GREET model. 2025 amounts: $0.637, $0.213, $0.159 and $0.127 per kg by tier; five times that with the prevailing wage and apprenticeship rules met.
  • Bought power. It counts as clean only with energy attribute certificates from new plants in the same region, matched by year until 2029 and by hour from 2030 (final rules, T.D. 10023, effective 10 January 2025).
  • Simplified here. One counting rule for all ten years; operating CO₂ in place of the rules’ life-cycle grid rate; no taxes, elective pay or transfers.
Go deeperWhat this model leaves out
  • Taxes, depreciation, debt and the financing schedule: the cash flows are the project's, before tax (A1).
  • Compression, storage, delivery and water: DOE's boundary for its cost per kg.
  • Uncertainty: one weather year, known prices, fixed rules (M7, M8).
  • Price-taking: the plant's buying and selling do not move Prairie Town's price.
  • Part-load limits and output that falls with age: the electrolyzer runs anywhere from 0 to its size (M4).

Check yourself

Myth or fact? “A credit of $3.185 per kg is worth $3.185 per kg of the plant's lifetime output.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. It is paid for 10 of 30 years: levelized over the life it is worth about $1.24/kg here. Counting it every year overstates the NPV by $129.9M.

Myth or fact? “Moving the boundary only changes the accounting, never the plan.”

  • Myth
  • Fact
Answer

Myth. Taking the increment changes only the accounting (46.2 MW either way), but moving the electrolyzer across the grid changes the plan: 40.1 MW, with every MWh paying the charge.

A plant costs $100M up front. At 4% real over 30 years, what is its annual equivalent?

  • $3.3M
  • $5.8M
  • $4.0M
  • $17.3M
Answer

$5.8M: the CRF is 0.04 ÷ (1 − 1.04−30) = 0.0578.

$3.3M is $100M ÷ 30 with no interest; $4.0M is interest alone; 17.3 is the annuity factor, in years.

In the real world

As the hydrogen price rose, the optimizer built a larger electrolyzer to use more surplus wind.

The larger unit ran at lower utilization, so each kilogram carried more capital and the levelized cost of hydrogen rose, while hydrogen revenue lowered total system cost.

From: Li et al. (2024), Journal of Mechanical Design.

Goal: the lowest life-cycle cost of the whole system, net of what it earns from electricity and hydrogen.

Where the result holds: one planned 253 MW wind farm in West Texas, with ERCOT wholesale prices; hydrogen sold at a set price per kilogram; cost assumptions dated 2020 to 2023.

It leaves out: final federal hydrogen credit rules, which came later; the rest of the grid, which enters only through wholesale prices; where the hydrogen goes after it is sold.

Case file CF2 tells the study's version: when the cost per kilogram and the cost of the system disagree.
