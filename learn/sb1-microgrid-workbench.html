Microgrid Workbench

Keep a fictional campus running on its own power: set what must stay on, size solar, a battery and diesel, and test hypothetical outages on 2025 solar profiles within the feeder’s limits

Sandbox SB1 of Energy Systems, Step by Step: set Prairie Campus’s load priorities, equipment, feeder limits, tariff and owner; a small mixed-integer program runs hypothetical outages hour by hour or sizes the plan.

The brief

The site
Prairie Campus (fictional, from F7): a county hospital, a water and heating plant, dorms and labs, 1,200 kW in all, on one feeder.
You decide
What must stay on; solar (kW), battery (kWh), 200 kW generator sets and diesel (gallons); the feeder's limits, the tariff and the owner.
The engine works out
A normal year on the grid, then each hypothetical outage hour by hour as an island, cut off from the main grid; or the cheapest sizes, with a small mixed-integer program.
It leaves out
Marginal electricity prices (the engine does not calculate them), the wiring inside the campus, how fuel use changes below full output, battery wear, and outages you do not choose.

Open-ended: start from a preset, change one thing at a time, and copy the plan link to keep a plan.

How the engine works, as diagrams

Run the outages
A linear program runs a normal year on 12 average days; then a mixed-integer program runs each outage as an island, with whole 200 kW sets that run at 30% or more.
Size it for me
One mixed-integer program picks the cheapest solar, battery and whole sets that keep every must-stay-on load on in every chosen outage. It leaves out the sets' 30% minimum.
Then
Its sizes, rounded up to the bench's steps, replace yours, and a run checks them with that minimum: a safe plan, not proven the cheapest on the steps.
It holds when
The chosen outages are the ones that matter, loads are flat and can be partly served, 12 average days stand in for the year, and the coming sun is known.
Each outage
Starts with a full battery and a full diesel tank; no fuel arrives during it.
It leaves out
Marginal prices, the campus's wiring, how fuel use changes below full output, battery wear, fuel deliveries, outage diesel in the annual cost, and how often outages happen.
Go deeper · ModelerThe model behind the bench

M5 writes the engine down, proves the sizing with branch and bound, and runs the storm on forecasts. Its notebook rebuilds the models in Python and checks the sizing and outage numbers.

Go deeperHow the outages are chosen

Every outage is hypothetical: the grid is assumed down while the 2025 sun of that stretch shines.

Named outage Grid assumed down
Winter storm, 3 days 72 hours from 00:00 on 20 January 2025
Winter evening, 1 day 24 hours from 16:00 on 20 January 2025
Summer storm, 3 days 72 hours from 00:00 on 28 July 2025

The ten drawn outages:

  • Seed 7: the same ten on every visit.
  • Start: any whole hour from 1 January to 25 February 2025, each equally likely.
  • Length: 6 to 72 hours, each equally likely.
  • A teaching sample, not outage statistics: riding through all ten is ten tests passed, nothing more.
Go deeperHow close 12 average days come to a full year

The normal year is 12 average days, one per month; each day's battery ends where it began. The test runs four plans over all 8,760 hours of 2025 as well:

Plan Grid bill, 12 average days Grid bill, 8,760 hours Difference
F7's campus (the default plan) · 600 kW, 2,000 kWh $1,117,962 $1,117,960 the same
Size it for me, from the default plan · 2,625 kW, 3,650 kWh $548,218 $571,684 +4.3%
Sized with the dorms also must stay on (C1) · 3,225 kW, 5,050 kWh $403,456 $430,228 +6.6%
M5's plan: exports capped at 0 kW · 2,575 kW, 3,600 kWh $562,709 $595,596 +5.8%

Average days smooth out runs of cloudy and sunny days, so a large solar-and-battery plan's bill comes out low. Other plans can differ more than these. Read the annual cost as an estimate.
