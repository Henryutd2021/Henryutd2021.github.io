United States focus; Canadian-filed vendor material is labeled as supplementary design context. Coverage: 1986/... January 1986 decommissioning-assurance cost bases, 2020–2025 fleet observations, 2026 policy and regulatory records, 2030-onward reference scenarios and construction-start phaseout cases through 2036 onward.

Values retain their source-specific bases; costs have not been escalated or converted. NR means not reported or not established, never zero. FOAK means first-of-a-kind; BOAK means between first- and nth-of-a-kind; NOAK means nth-of-a-kind. Blank numeric fields are missing values. Suffixes separate scenarios or years while retaining the common N-number. Verification of a printed value does not establish realized cost, performance or eligibility.

The paper column labels input use, citation provenance, numerical correspondence and contextual discussion separately. Input use identifies a stated model assumption; it does not establish the original source. Context is not a numerical input. Only examined public versions are linked, with version-specific locators. All expanded basis and locator text is present in the page and downloads.

Read before using

Overnight versus total capital
Record IDC/AFUDC, escalation during construction, owner’s costs, contingency, initial fuel and interconnection separately. A table labelled “Total Capital Cost” may still be an overnight total.
FOAK versus BOAK versus NOAK
Preserve the source’s term. Distinguish first design, first country deployment, first site unit and repeat factory production.
$/kWe versus $/kWth
Never substitute one for the other without an explicit efficiency and boundary.
Net versus gross output
Record auxiliary demand and whether the denominator is generator output, plant export or delivered customer power.
Capacity factor versus availability
Fleet CF includes generation behavior; availability describes whether equipment can operate. Neither alone establishes data-center service reliability.
Ramp rate versus operating envelope
Identify rated-power denominator, electric versus thermal power, permitted range, time resolution and fuel-cycle conditions.
Design life versus licence term
Keep technical life, amortization life, analysis horizon and regulatory permission in separate fields.
Fuel price versus fuel cost
Uranium and SWU purchase prices do not determine $/MWh without enrichment, tails, fabrication, burnup, efficiency and inventory assumptions.
Production versus investment credit
§45Y applies to qualifying production; §48E applies to qualified investment. Percentage bonuses and percentage-point adders differ.
Reactor temperature versus usable heat
Include steam pressure, phase, extraction location, return conditions and temperature losses.
Footprint versus site
Separate building envelope, protected area, owner-controlled area, construction laydown, cooling systems and EPZ.
Vendor claim versus reviewed value
Government hosting does not make vendor content a government endorsement. Design approval does not establish commercial performance.
Decommissioning assurance versus expenditure
A regulatory minimum funding formula is not a complete project cost estimate.
Historical order versus current entitlement
A dated FERC order cannot establish today’s contractual limit without checking subsequent proceedings.
Verification and source conflicts
Transcription verification confirms what a document prints. The separate underlying-evidence and conflict fields describe the limits of that evidence. Neither a model input nor a vendor design claim is independently demonstrated operating performance.
Policy scope and payment
Qualifying capacity additions at older facilities can fall within §45Y/§48E. Domestic-content requirements can affect elective payment as well as bonus eligibility; statutory exceptions and waivers must be checked.

Edition, revision, release date, retrieval date and document SHA-256 are separate fields. A checksum identifies retrieved bytes, not accuracy; live HTML navigation can change without changing a cited table.
